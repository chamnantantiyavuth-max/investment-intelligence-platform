JNJ's Talc Proposal: A Conditional Schedule, Not Finality

JNJ's proposed $5.5B ovarian-talc resolution is consistent with an attempt to compress a multi-decade legal overhang into a scheduled, financeable obligation — but it is not finality: participation-gated, cash-deferred, accounting-unproven. The CRO dissents: cleanup exists only after participation, recognition, residual dockets, and capital commitments execute together.

Article treatment — typeset feature

The participation gate is the first filter

At least 95% of the 76,000 ovarian-talc claims must elect in for the resolution to bind. That is a participation schedule, not a settlement in hand — and it is the first of several conditions that must execute together before any cleanup exists.

“Cleanup exists only after participation, recognition, residual dockets, and capital commitments execute together.”

Cash is deferred: the first payment of no more than $3B lands in 2027. The $3.7B Q2-26 reserve predates the proposal and is accounting-unproven against it. Residual channels remain — mesothelioma, Canada, securities class action.
