MATERIAL FINDINGS:
1. Evidence admission is not consistently bound to a verified run/candidate/source.
   (a) `qad/m6/source_bridge.py:892-933` makes `verification` optional and only checks its full run/candidate/source binding when the EAR claims `original_source_verified`. Otherwise it trusts the caller-supplied `verified_source_id`. At `:855-860`, the builder checks only that a verdict is minted and matches the source—not the run/candidate. The existing registry gate at `qad/persistence/reference.py:1217-1256` checks the EAR/EV identity, source existence, and the AI-method `"true"` value, but not the M6.6 verdict binding. The executed probe showed: the bridge helper accepted an EAR with no verdict for `NO-SUCH-RUN` / `NO-SUCH-CANDIDATE` and admitted its EV; a verdict for L-1/candidate-1 also produced an EAR with `"true"` that direct `registry.admit_evidence` accepted for another run. The proof-bound bridge helper itself rejected that cross-run verdict.
   (b) This allows canonical evidence state—and, through the direct registry boundary, canonical `original_source_verified="true"`—without verification for the exact run/candidate/source.
   (c) Require a minted verdict bound to the exact run/candidate/source for every M6.6 evidence admission, not only when the EAR asserts `"true"`. Ensure the canonical admission path cannot accept M6.6’s true claim based only on the string.
   (d) Add regressions proving an absent verdict and nonexistent run/candidate cause zero writes, a cross-run verdict is rejected through every canonical admission path, and the matching run/candidate/source still succeeds.

2. Cross-store failure recovery loses canonical evidence lineage.
   (a) In `qad/m6/source_bridge.py:1038-1078`, canonical evidence is admitted before the ledger disposition. I injected a one-time failure in `dispose_candidate` after EV/EAR admission. On re-entry, the registry returned `IntegrityConflict` for the already-admitted EV; the bridge then persisted a disposition with empty `evidence_ids` even though the canonical EV still existed. Probe output confirmed the mismatch.
   (b) The durable ledger can say evidence admission failed and omit EV/EAR references after those canonical records were actually committed; re-entry is therefore not truthful or idempotent for this partial-failure case.
   (c) Reconcile already-admitted EV/EAR records on re-entry: verify their stored identities/payloads match the rebuilt records, recover their IDs, then persist the disposition. Do not record an empty-link failure when matching canonical evidence exists.
   (d) Add a failure-injection test that fails disposition persistence after evidence admission, retries, and asserts the durable disposition references the existing EV/EAR with no false failure reason.
