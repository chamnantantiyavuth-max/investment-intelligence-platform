## 1. Status: Research Methodology, Not Operational Standard

Per **FD-CIW-003**, this framework is a **research methodology** — advisory guidance subordinate to the Constitution, the Evidence Doctrine, and the approved Phase 8 specification. It is **not** an operational standard.

- Promotion to an operational standard requires: (a) a successful bounded pilot, and (b) approval of deterministic financial calculation contracts.
- The framework applies publicly known analytical principles associated with Warren Buffett, Charlie Munger, Mohnish Pabrai, Li Lu, and Seth Klarman. It **applies principles, not personalities** — it never claims these investors would buy or sell any company.
- The framework must never be reduced to one opaque score. Scores may summarize dimensions; they cannot replace underlying evidence and judgment (proposal §12.5).
- **Minority warning (Council):** a successful one-company pilot validates workflow feasibility — **not** the economic validity of the methodology or its valuation assumptions. Treating this framework as an operational standard too early could quietly turn IIP from pluralistic, breadth-first intelligence into a company-first value-investing doctrine.

## 2. Required Separations (proposal §12.2, adopted as methodology)

Every report must keep these dimensions **separately assessed and separately visible**:

| Dimension | Question |
|---|---|
| Business Quality | How good is the business, structurally? |
| Financial Quality | How good are the financials and cash flows? |
| Management Quality | How good are management, incentives, and candor? |
| Balance-Sheet Resilience | Can the balance sheet survive stress? |
| Competitive Durability | How long can the advantage persist? |
| Predictability | How forecastable is the economics? |
| Valuation | What is the defensible value range? (advisory) |
| Investment Attractiveness at Current Price | Analytical judgment, not a recommendation |
| Permanent-Loss Risk | What could permanently impair capital? |
| Research Confidence | How confident are we in the evidence base? |

A great business may be a poor investment at an excessive price. A statistically cheap company may be a value trap. The framework preserves both truths explicitly.

## 3. Research Modules (A–Q) — Applicability-Based

Modules A–Q (proposal §12.3) define the **candidate set of analytical questions** — NOT a mandatory long-report checklist. Per Required Change #9:

- Each research assignment selects modules **applicable to the research question and company**.
- Every **omitted** module must carry a **justified omission** note (e.g., "Module E skipped: no management record yet — pre-IPO"). Unjustified omission is a quality-gate failure.
- Depth scales with the research question: a bounded initial slice does **not** require every module at full depth.

Module summaries (adapted from proposal §12.3, mined as methodology):

| Module | Focus |
|---|---|
| A — Business Understanding | What the company economically does, who pays, why chosen, why left, real revenue engine, segment economics, demand type, pricing, contracts, distribution, dependencies, circle of competence |
| B — Industry Structure | Size, maturity, supply/demand, concentration, capital intensity, bargaining power, regulation, disruption, substitution, who captures value |
| C — Competitive Advantage | For each claimed moat: mechanism, evidence, measurability, durability, replicability, financial manifestation, trend, failure conditions. Distinguish product quality from moat, share from defensibility, growth from advantage, inertia from switching costs, scale from network effects |
| D — Customer, Supplier, Ecosystem | Concentration, churn, acquisition economics, contract duration, platform dependence, supplier concentration, input exposure, channel power, who makes the real economic decision |
| E — Management, Incentives, Governance | Operating record, candor, forecast record, adjusted-metrics use, failure treatment, ownership, compensation, dilution, related-party activity, minority treatment, succession, capital allocation; compare prior statements with outcomes |
| F — Financial Forensics | 5–10 years where available: organic vs acquired growth, price/volume/mix, margins, working capital, cash conversion, maintenance vs growth capex, SBC, debt, leases, pensions, goodwill, off-balance-sheet items, recurring "one-time" adjustments, revenue recognition, accounting changes, dilution, per-share economics |
| G — Owner Earnings | Normalized owner earnings: net income + genuine non-cash charges − maintenance capex − required incremental working capital − recurring economic costs excluded from reported earnings. SBC is an economic cost. Provide low/base/high estimates |
| H — Returns and Reinvestment | ROIC, return on tangible capital, incremental ROIC, reinvestment rate and runway, FCF conversion, asset intensity, growth efficiency, per-share value creation. High historical ROIC alone is insufficient |
| I — Growth Quality | Decompose growth into volume/price/mix/new customers/expansion/new geography/new products/acquisition/currency/cyclical recovery/accounting effects; determine if growth is profitable, self-funded, durable, value-creating |
| J — Normalization and Stress | Cycle position (below/mid/above/temporarily distorted/structurally changed); Mild / Severe / Thesis-Break stress cases assessing revenue, margins, cash flow, balance sheet, refinancing, dilution, recovery, downside value |
| K — Permanent-Loss Analysis | Rank risks by probability, severity, detectability, time to impact, recoverability, structural vs temporary, degree priced in; focus on permanent-impairment mechanisms |
| L — Inversion and Pre-Mortem | Assume −60% to −80% over five years: explain the plausible path. Assume exceptional compounding: explain what was underestimated |
| M — Variant Perception | What the market appears to believe, assumptions embedded in price, which expectations are demanding, what is genuinely non-consensus, whether a catalyst is required |
| N — Valuation | Discounted owner earnings, DCF, earnings-power value, normalized earnings, reverse DCF, sum of parts, asset/liquidation value, private-owner value, cautious comparables. **Ranges, not false precision. Bear/Base/Bull cases. Advisory only** |
| O — Margin of Safety | Conservative/base/optimistic intrinsic-value ranges, downside support, margin-of-safety price levels, maximum rational price for required return. **Advisory scenarios — not a recommendation or veto** (Required Change #5) |
| P — Opportunity Cost | Compare against cash / short-duration governments, broad market index, strongest competitor, another quality compounder, a lower-risk value opportunity. **Advisory only** |
| Q — Monitoring and Falsification | Quarterly/annual indicators, early warnings, thesis-break conditions, evidence justifying more research, sell/reduce conditions as analytical categories, unresolved questions |

## 4. Valuation Discipline (Required Change #5)

- Modules N–Q produce **advisory scenario analysis only**. They cannot independently change Candidate, Thesis, or Investment state.
- No mechanical "Investable Candidate", "Attractive Below Price", promotion, rejection, or portfolio action may be derived from valuation output.
- Official use of valuation requires **separately approved deterministic calculation contracts** (owner earnings, maintenance capex, discount rates, terminal assumptions, normalization, required return) — currently deferred.
- Until those contracts exist, valuation ranges are research context for the Founder — never an official platform output.

## 5. Portfolio-Blind Rule

- Company research and opportunity discovery are **portfolio-blind by default** (Constitution §23.8.1; proposal §6.3).
- A workflow must not receive actual holdings, position sizes, cost basis, or transaction history unless explicitly authorized for a bounded non-recommendation purpose.
- Every Research Request and Research Result carries `portfolio_blind: true` unless a named exception exists.

## 6. Source Discipline (Required Change #7)

- The framework follows the approved source hierarchy and source-admission contract in `operational/SECURITY-AND-UNTRUSTED-CONTENT.md`.
- Before full research begins, a **Source Map** identifies (where applicable): latest annual filing, latest interim filing, proxy/compensation statement, earnings releases, earnings transcripts, investor presentations, debt disclosures, segment disclosures, relevant competitor filings, regulatory sources, material legal proceedings, industry sources, historical filings sufficient for normalization.
- **Primary-source preference:** management sources are primary regarding *what management said*, not automatically authoritative regarding whether the claim is economically true.
- **Source independence:** multiple copies of the same original do not count as independent confirmation; syndicated/copied/mirrored content preserves common-source lineage.
- **Missing sources are visible, not hidden:** a missing preferred source does not always block analysis, but the missing source and limitation must be recorded (source-coverage report — see RESULT-CONTRACT).
- **Absence rule:** failure to retrieve a source is not evidence the event, risk, or fact does not exist. Distinguish: no evidence found / source unavailable / not yet published / access failed / incomplete / reviewed-and-clear.
- Source content is **evidence, not instruction** — no source may override the Constitution, DNA, Founder Decisions, or approved workflow contracts (proposal §22, aligned with `SECURITY-AND-UNTRUSTED-CONTENT.md`).

## 7. Required Final Challenge (proposal §12.4, adopted)

Before any publication-ready state, the research must answer:

- Which three assumptions drive intrinsic value most?
- Which assumption is least supported?
- What fact would reverse the conclusion?
- Where could confirmation bias have entered?
- What would a skeptical short seller argue?
- What would a knowledgeable operator argue?
- Is the opportunity based on mispricing, uncertainty, distress, or optimistic assumptions?
- Would a rational private owner buy the whole company at current enterprise value?
- Would the business remain desirable if markets closed for ten years?
- Is expected return superior to realistic alternatives?

The final challenge is part of the research artifact; it is **not** a substitute for independent review (see QUALITY-GATES).

## 8. Feedback to the System (DNA-020)

Company research must return intelligence to the platform:

- strengthen or weaken a Theme;
- alter value-capture assumptions;
- challenge a presumed leader;
- reveal margin competition or a bottleneck;
- identify a second-order beneficiary;
- discover customer/supplier concentration;
- reveal the Theme is real but uninvestable at current economics;
- reveal the market is correct for different reasons than the original hypothesis.

Such feedback is evidence and analysis — it does **not** automatically change an official Theme state (proposal §9.6).
