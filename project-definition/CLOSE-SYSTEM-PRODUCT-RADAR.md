## 1. Strategy Identity

### 1.1 What It Is

Close System Product Radar is an **opportunity discovery strategy** within the Momentum & Market Leadership intelligence path. It scans for ETF, Commodity, and Index products that meet the Close System philosophy: buy structurally sound assets at a discount, hold for extended periods, and let capital allocation manage risk — not stop-losses.

It answers one question:

> Which ETF, Commodity, or Index products currently trade at a discount to their structural value — supported by real-world demand and a natural price floor — and therefore merit deep investigation?

It does **not** answer:

- How much to allocate
- When to exit
- How to size the position
- What stop-loss to set

Those questions belong to **Capital Command** (external system).

### 1.2 Philosophy

| Principle | Description |
|---|---|
| **Capital Preservation by Product Selection** | Risk is managed by choosing assets that cannot go to zero by their nature — not by stop-losses |
| **Discount Entry** | Entry advantage comes from buying when fear, cyclical troughs, or policy shocks create temporary mispricing |
| **Structural Demand** | The asset must serve a real, enduring economic purpose — not speculative narrative |
| **Patience** | Holding periods are measured in years, not weeks. The strategy outlasts volatility |
| **Macro Awareness** | Global macro, government policy, and hidden market signals inform opportunity identification — not just price charts |

## 2. Product Eligibility — Three Criteria

A product must satisfy **all three criteria** to enter the Close System radar. Partial satisfaction is not sufficient.

### 2.1 Criterion P1: Cannot Go to Zero by Nature

The product's structure, underlying assets, or physical reality prevents a total loss of value. This excludes individual equities (which can go to zero through bankruptcy) and includes:

| Product Type | Why It Cannot Go to Zero |
|---|---|
| **Broad ETFs** (SPY, QQQ, IWM) | Represent diversified baskets of hundreds of companies — total loss requires systemic economic collapse |
| **Sector ETFs** (XLF, XLE, XLP) | Represent entire economic sectors — demand for energy, food, or finance does not disappear |
| **Physical Commodities** (Gold, Oil, Copper, Wheat) | Have intrinsic industrial or monetary utility — even at cyclical lows, physical goods retain some value |
| **Rare Earth / Strategic Minerals** | Required for industrial processes with no near-term substitutes — demand floor exists |
| **Commodity Producer ETFs** (GDX, XME, REMX) | Diversified producers with hard asset backing — individual miners can fail, but the sector cannot |
| **Sovereign Bonds / TIPS** | Backed by sovereign credit — can lose purchasing power but not go to zero barring default |
| **Agricultural Commodities** | Food demand is inelastic — population growth provides structural consumption floor |

**Excluded by P1:**
- Individual equities (single-company bankruptcy risk)
- Leveraged/inverse ETFs (decay can approach zero)
- Options and derivatives (expiry, counterparty risk)
- Cryptocurrencies without institutional adoption history (speculative value floor unproven)
- Synthetic/structured products without physical backing

### 2.2 Criterion P2: Discount Pricing

The product trades at a meaningful discount driven by fear, cyclical factors, or temporary dislocation — not by permanent structural impairment.

| Discount Signal | What to Look For | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Production Cost Proximity** | Commodity price within 10-15% of marginal producer's all-in sustaining cost (AISC) | Gold at $1,200 when AISC is $1,100 — miners losing money, supply will contract |
| **Fear-Driven Selloff** | VIX spike, credit spread widening, sentiment extremes (AAII, put/call) with unchanged fundamentals | SPY -20% on recession fear while corporate earnings still growing |
| **Cyclical Trough** | Commodity at multi-year low while demand trajectory intact | Oil at $45 when global GDP growing and inventory drawing |
| **Policy Shock Discount** | Government action creates temporary oversupply or demand suppression | Trade tariff announcements causing sector ETF to gap down 15% |
| **Sentiment vs. Fundamentals Divergence** | Negative narrative (headlines, analyst downgrades) while underlying data (supply/demand, cost, inventory) remains constructive | "Oil is dead" narrative while global consumption hits record highs |
| **Dislocation** | Futures curve in backwardation or contango extremes signaling temporary supply/demand imbalance | Spot gold premium over futures — physical shortage, not paper weakness |

**What is NOT a discount:**
- A stock that fell 90% because the business model is broken (structural impairment, not discount)
- A commodity declining because of permanent demand destruction (coal, thermal)
- An ETF down because its underlying holdings are deteriorating (not fear — fundamentals)

### 2.3 Criterion P3: Real-World Structural Demand

The product's underlying asset serves an enduring economic function. Demand is driven by consumption, production, or infrastructure — not speculation, narrative, or hype.

| Demand Signal | What to Look For | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Industrial Consumption** | The asset is a physical input to manufacturing, construction, energy, or technology | Copper for electrification, Silver for solar panels, Rare earth for magnets |
| **Inelastic Demand** | Consumption does not significantly decline with price increases — people still need food, energy, shelter | Wheat, Natural Gas, Water Infrastructure |
| **Infrastructure Backing** | Government or institutional capital expenditure programs create multi-decade demand visibility | Grid modernization → Copper, Transmission → Steel/Aluminum |
| **Secular Trend Alignment** | Demand trajectory supported by demographic, technological, or policy megatrends | Aging population → Healthcare ETFs, Energy transition → Lithium/Copper |
| **Monetary/Store-of-Value** | The asset serves as a recognized store of value or hedge against currency debasement | Gold, TIPS, inflation-protected bonds |
| **Supply Constraints** | Limited new supply due to geological scarcity, permitting timelines (5-10 years for new mines), or geopolitical concentration | Rare earth processing (90% China), Platinum Group Metals (90% South Africa/Russia) |

**What is NOT structural demand:**
- Meme-driven or social-media-hyped asset flows (unsustainable)
- Purely speculative demand with no physical consumption (NFTs, most crypto in 2021)
- Temporary policy subsidy without long-term economic rationale

## 4. Intelligence Layers — How Opportunities Are Found

The radar operates across five intelligence layers, each providing a different lens. The power of the system comes from **connective reasoning** across layers — not from any single data point.

```

    ↓

### 4.1 Layer 1: Macro Economics

**Purpose:** Identify the macroeconomic regime to determine which asset classes are in favor and whether risk appetite supports commodity/ETF exposure.

| Data Point | Source Type | Signal |
|---|---|---|
| **Real Interest Rates** (Nominal - Inflation) | Central bank data, CPI, bond yields | Negative real rates → gold/commodities attractive; rising real rates → headwind |
| **Dollar Index (DXY)** | ICE, Fed data | Weakening dollar → commodity tailwind (priced in USD); strengthening → headwind |
| **Global PMI** (Manufacturing + Services) | S&P Global, ISM, Caixin | PMI > 50 expanding → industrial commodity demand rising |
| **Yield Curve** (2s10s spread) | Treasury data | Inversion → recession risk → defensive positioning; steepening → growth → cyclicals |
| **Inflation Trajectory** (CPI, PPI, PCE) | BLS, BEA | Rising inflation → commodity/hard asset support; falling → growth/Tech ETF support |
| **Central Bank Policy** (Fed, ECB, PBoC rate path) | FOMC, ECB, PBoC | Cutting cycle → liquidity expansion → risk assets; hiking → contraction |
| **Credit Spreads** (HY OAS, IG OAS) | FRED, Bloomberg | Widening → risk-off → fear discount opportunity for ETFs |
| **Global Trade Volumes** | WTO, CPB, Baltic Dry Index | Rising → commodity demand healthy; falling → demand concern |

**Connective reasoning example:**
> Dollar weakening + global PMI expanding + credit spreads stable → commodity-friendly regime. Narrow radar to commodity products for discount evaluation.

### 4.2 Layer 2: Government Policy

**Purpose:** Detect policy actions that create supply disruptions or demand catalysts — often before markets fully price them in.

| Policy Signal | What to Watch | Impact |
|---|---|---|
| **Strategic Reserve Activity** | SPR releases/refills (US), gold reserve purchases (PBoC, RBI, Turkey) | Reserve buying → physical demand floor; SPR release → temporary supply, buying opportunity when refill announced |
| **Export Restrictions** | China rare earth quotas, Indonesia nickel ban, Russia fertilizer limits | Supply contraction → price support for non-restricted producers |
| **Sanctions/Tariffs** | OFAC, EU sanctions, Section 301 tariffs | Supply chain disruption → alternative supplier ETFs benefit |
| **Industrial Policy** | CHIPS Act, IRA, EU Green Deal, REPowerEU | Multi-year capex → structural demand for input commodities |
| **Climate/Environmental Regulation** | Carbon pricing, methane rules, water usage permits | Raises production cost → higher price floor; restricts new supply |
| **Subsidy Programs** | EV tax credits, solar/wind subsidies, agricultural supports | Artificial demand boost — assess sustainability when subsidy expires |
| **Central Bank Gold Agreements** | CBGA limits, IMF gold sales | Central bank selling → temporary supply; buying → structural demand |
| **Strategic Stockpiling** | National food/energy security reserves (China grain, Japan oil) | Hidden demand — not reported through normal trade channels |

**Connective reasoning example:**
> EU Critical Raw Materials Act mandates 10% domestic mining + 40% domestic processing by 2030. China concurrently reduces rare earth export quotas by 20%. → Non-China rare earth producers (MP Materials, Lynas) face structural demand increase + supply gap. Cross-reference with Layer 3: are rare earth prices near production cost?

### 4.3 Layer 3: Cost Structure

**Purpose:** Identify the price floor — the level at which production becomes unprofitable and supply contracts, creating a natural support.

| Cost Metric | What It Tells You | Data Source |
|---|---|---|
| **All-In Sustaining Cost (AISC)** | Full cost to produce one unit including sustaining capex — for miners/producers | Company filings, S&P Global, Wood Mackenzie |
| **Marginal Cost Curve** | Cost of the highest-cost producer currently supplying the market | Industry reports, consulting studies |
| **Breakeven Price** | Price at which the average producer covers all costs including cost of capital | Analyst estimates, producer guidance |
| **Cash Cost (C1)** | Direct production cost only — for near-term survival floor | Company reports |
| **Production Cost Trend** | Is cost rising (inflation, depletion, regulation) or falling (technology, scale)? | Industry data, inflation metrics |
| **Shut-In Price** | Price below which producers stop producing (not just lose money) | Historical production data during prior downturns |

**Signal strength hierarchy:**
1. **Strongest:** Current price ≤ marginal producer's cash cost → supply contraction inevitable
2. **Strong:** Current price ≤ AISC for majority of producers → industry unprofitable → capex cuts → future supply deficit
3. **Moderate:** Current price ≤ AISC for highest-cost quartile → marginal producers bleeding
4. **Weak:** Current price above all producers' AISC → no cost-based floor

**Connective reasoning example:**
> Gold miners' average AISC = $1,350/oz. Gold currently at $1,450. Marginal producers (highest-cost quartile) at $1,500 AISC — they're losing money. If gold stays below $1,500 for 2 more quarters → supply contraction (mine closures, exploration cuts). This creates a structural floor AND future supply deficit. GDX (gold miners ETF) likely discounting this.

### 4.4 Layer 4: Supply/Demand Balance

**Purpose:** Identify imbalances between supply and demand — the engine of commodity price cycles.

| Supply Signal | What to Watch |
|---|---|
| **Inventory Levels** | Exchange inventories (LME, COMEX, SHFE), producer stockpiles, strategic reserves — declining = tightening |
| **Capacity Utilization** | Mine/factory utilization rate — above 90% = supply-constrained |
| **New Project Pipeline** | Permitted, under-construction, feasibility-stage projects — thin pipeline = future supply gap |
| **Permitting Timelines** | Average years from discovery to production (5-10 for mines) — no quick supply response |
| **Depletion Rates** | Existing mines declining at 3-5%/year → constant new supply needed just to stay flat |
| **Geopolitical Concentration** | % of global supply from top 3 countries — >70% = concentration risk |
| **Recycling/Secondary Supply** | Scrap recovery rates — can fill gap but limited by collection infrastructure |

| Demand Signal | What to Watch |
|---|---|
| **Consumption Growth Rate** | Annual demand growth % — is it accelerating or decelerating? |
| **Demand Elasticity** | Does demand drop when price rises? (Oil: inelastic; Copper: moderately elastic) |
| **Substitution Risk** | Can users switch to alternatives? (Platinum vs Palladium in catalytic converters) |
| **New Use Cases** | Emerging applications creating incremental demand (Silver in solar, Copper in EVs) |
| **Regional Demand Shift** | Is demand growing in China, India, SE Asia while flat in developed markets? |
| **Stockpiling Behavior** | Are end-users building inventory (bullish) or drawing down (bearish)? |

**Connective reasoning example:**
> Copper: Global mine supply growing at 1.5%/year (depleting mines, few new projects). Demand growing at 3.5%/year (electrification, EVs, grid). Structural deficit of ~500K tons/year by 2026. Exchange inventories at 5-year lows. Copper price at $3.80/lb, AISC for top producers ~$2.50 → NOT at discount. Wait for macro scare (Layer 1) to push copper below $3.00 — then it enters Close System radar.

### 4.5 Layer 5: Hidden Signals

**Purpose:** Detect market activity that is not reflected in headline prices — physical market dynamics, institutional positioning, and trade flow anomalies that precede price discovery.

| Hidden Signal | What to Watch | Why It Matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Physical vs Paper Divergence** | Spot physical premium over futures, ETF physical metal holdings vs futures open interest | Physical tightness while paper market discounts — real demand exceeds financial pricing |
| **Central Bank Activity** | Gold purchases by PBoC, RBI, Turkey, Poland — reported quarterly with delay | Central banks are price-insensitive buyers — they accumulate regardless of price |
| **Trade Flow Anomalies** | Import/export data showing unusual patterns — China importing 3x normal copper volume | Someone is building inventory quietly — foreshadows supply concern or demand surge |
| **Coin/Bar Premiums** | Retail physical premiums over spot (American Eagles, Krugerrands, gold bars) | Retail/physical demand diverging from futures price — "smart money" accumulating |
| **Shipping/Freight Signals** | Baltic Dry Index divergence, port congestion at key commodity export hubs, vessel tracking data | Physical goods movement confirming or contradicting reported trade data |
| **Producer Hedging Behavior** | Mining company hedge books — are producers locking in prices (bearish) or reducing hedges (bullish)? | Producers have the best supply visibility — their hedging tells you what they expect |
| **ETF Flow vs Price Divergence** | GDX price flat but +$500M inflows over 4 weeks | Institutional accumulation before price moves |
| **Options Market Signals** | Deep OTM call buying in commodity ETFs, put/call skew extremes | Smart money positioning for upside — or hedging against tail risk |
| **Patent/Technology Signals** | New extraction technology (lithium DLE, copper bio-leaching), recycling breakthroughs | Could change supply curve — reduces scarcity premium |

**Connective reasoning example:**
> Silver: COMEX futures at $22/oz (weak). But US Mint silver Eagle sales +40% YoY, Indian silver imports at record, and Shanghai silver premium $2/oz over London. Physical market is tight while paper market doesn't reflect it. PBoC not reporting silver purchases (unlike gold) but trade data shows Chinese silver imports surging. The paper price is a discount to physical reality. → Close System opportunity when silver dips on paper selling.

## 5. Connective Reasoning Framework

Opportunities are not identified by any single layer. The radar's power is in **cross-layer synthesis** — confirmation across multiple independent intelligence sources.

### 5.1 Conviction Scale

| Conviction | Layers Aligned | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **Low** | 1-2 layers signal, others neutral or unclear | Add to radar watchlist — monitor for additional confirmation |
| **Moderate** | 3 layers confirm, 1 contradicting | Deep research — build full thesis with evidence references |
| **High** | 4+ layers confirm, no material contradiction | Present to Founder for review — product meets criteria |
| **Maximum** | All 5 layers confirm + hidden signals corroborate + discount confirmed | Priority presentation — rare alignment |

### 5.2 Synthesis Template

Every Close System opportunity assessment follows this structure:

```
Product: {name} ({ticker})
Category: {Broad ETF / Sector ETF / Commodity / Fixed Income}
Date: {assessment date}

## Discount Assessment (Criterion P2)
- Current price: {price}
- Production cost / AISC: {cost}
- Discount signal: {fear-driven / cyclical trough / policy shock / cost proximity}
- Discount magnitude: {price as % of cost or historical valuation metric}
- Evidence: {specific data points with sources}

## Demand Assessment (Criterion P3)
- Demand type: {industrial / inelastic / infrastructure / secular trend / monetary}
- Demand trajectory: {growth rate, forecast}
- Supply constraints: {concentration, depletion, permitting}
- Evidence: {specific data points with sources}

## Cross-Layer Synthesis
| Layer | Signal | Alignment |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Macro | {regime, risk appetite} | {supporting / neutral / contradicting} |
| 2. Policy | {relevant policy} | {supporting / neutral / contradicting} |

## 7. Relationship to Alpha Momentum

Close System Product Radar and Alpha Momentum are **peer strategies** within the Momentum & Market Leadership intelligence path. They share the Shared Core but operate independently.

| Dimension | Alpha Momentum | Close System Product Radar |
|---|---|---|
| **Philosophy** | Momentum — what is working now | Value — what is cheap relative to structural worth |
| **Product Universe** | Individual stocks (US-listed common stocks + ADRs) | ETFs, Commodities, Indices, Fixed Income |
| **Time Horizon** | Weeks to months | Years |
| **Entry Signal** | Stage 2 breakout, technical setup | Discount to production cost, fear-driven mispricing |
| **Risk Approach** | Technical exit signals | Product selection + position sizing (via Capital Command) |
| **Data Focus** | Price, relative strength, earnings, theme evidence | Macro, policy, cost curves, supply/demand, hidden flows |
| **Conviction Drivers** | Theme strength + leadership + entry readiness | Discount depth + structural demand + cross-layer alignment |
| **Output** | Research Queue (theme-first) | Product Radar (discount-first) |

They may identify the same underlying asset through different lenses. Example:

- **Alpha Momentum:** GDX is breaking out of a Stage 1 base on high volume, gold theme strengthening → Enter watchlist
- **Close System Radar:** GDX holdings trading below AISC, gold at discount to inflation-adjusted average, central banks accumulating → Deep research opportunity

Both perspectives are valid — they answer different questions for different holding periods.
