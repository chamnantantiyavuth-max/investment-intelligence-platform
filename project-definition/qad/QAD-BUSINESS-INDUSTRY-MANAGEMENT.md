## 2. Business Quality Framework

### 2.1 Business Anatomy

Understand the business model at its core:

- What does the company actually do? (product/service, customer, value proposition)
- How does it make money? (unit economics, revenue model, pricing)
- Who pays? (customer concentration, switching costs)
- Who decides? (purchasing decision-maker, sales cycle)
- Why do customers stay? (retention, stickiness, switching costs)
- How does the business scale? (marginal cost curve, capacity, reinvestment)

### 2.2 Customer Economics

- Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC)
- Customer Lifetime Value (LTV)
- LTV / CAC ratio
- Payback period
- Churn / retention rate
- Revenue per customer
- Unit economics trends

### 2.3 Moat Analysis (6-Area Qualitative Framework per FD #61)

| Moat Type | Key Questions |
|-----------|--------------|
| **Switching Costs** | How hard/costly is it for customers to leave? Are there data, integration, workflow, or contractual moats? |
| **Pricing Power** | Can the company raise prices without losing customers? What evidence exists? |
| **Scale / Efficiency** | Does being larger create a structural cost advantage? Is this advantage widening or narrowing? |
| **Network Effects** | Does each new user add value for existing users? Are there multi-sided network dynamics? |
| **Distribution / Access** | Does the company control access to customers, shelf space, or distribution channels? |
| **Brand / Reputation** | Does brand drive premium pricing, preference, or customer trust? Is the brand durable? |
| **Regulatory / IP Advantage** | Does regulation, patents, or IP create a protected position? How long will it last? |

### 2.4 Moat Dimensions

| Dimension | Assessment | Evidence |
|-----------|------------|----------|
| **Mechanism** | What type of moat? | Qualitative analysis |
| **Width** | How large is the advantage? | Narrow / Moderate / Wide |
| **Depth** | How embedded is it? | Shallow / Moderate / Deep |
| **Trend** | Is the moat strengthening or weakening? | Strengthening / Stable / Weakening / At Risk |
| **Durability** | How long will it likely persist? | Years / Decade+ / Uncertain |

### 2.5 Quality Verification States

| State | Meaning | Criteria |
|-------|---------|----------|
| **VERIFIED** | High confidence in quality | Multiple independent evidence streams confirm moat, customer economics, and durability |
| **PROBABLE** | Likely high quality; some evidence gaps | Core moat evidence present but some dimensions unresolved |
| **UNRESOLVED** | Quality unclear; material evidence gaps | Insufficient evidence to determine quality; gaps identified |
| **FAILED** | Not a high-quality business | Evidence confirms moat is weak, absent, or structural advantage is deteriorating |

### 2.6 False-Quality Test

Every quality assessment must explicitly consider:

- Is this a genuinely good business, or just a business in a good cycle?
- Is the moat real, or does the company operate in a structurally protected niche that masks weakness?
- Are returns on capital genuinely high, or inflated by financial leverage, one-time gains, or understated assets?
- Is the growth high-quality (unit-economics-positive, reinvestment rich) or low-quality (dilutive, acquisition-driven, one-time)?

**Quality without this test is incomplete.**

### 2.7 Reinvestment Runway

- How much can the company reinvest at high returns?
- What is the incremental ROIC trend?
- What is the TAM (total addressable market) and the company's share?
- Is there room to grow within the existing moat, or would growth dilute moat? (moat vs growth tension)

## 3. Industry Economics Framework

Use the following analytical chain:

```text
DEMAND → SUPPLY → CAPACITY → UTILIZATION → PRICING → MARGINS → ROIC → CAPITAL ENTRY/EXIT → FUTURE CAPACITY
```

### 3.1 Industry Structure

| Element | Questions |
|---------|-----------|
| **Demand** | What drives demand? Is it growing, stable, or declining? Cyclical or secular? |
| **Supply** | Who supplies? Is supply concentrated or fragmented? Structure of supply? |
| **Capacity** | Is the industry at, above, or below capacity? How long to add new capacity? |
| **Utilization** | Current utilization trends? Is utilization improving or deteriorating? |
| **Pricing** | How is price determined? Commodity vs differentiated? Pricing power vs price-taking? |
| **Margins** | What are normal margins? Are margins above/below normal? Trend? |
| **ROIC** | What does industry ROIC look like? Is capital attracted or leaving? |
| **Capital Entry/Exit** | Are new entrants coming? Are incumbents leaving? Capital cycle phase? |
| **Future Capacity** | What capacity is being built? What is the supply pipeline? |

### 3.2 Porter's Five Forces (Contextual)

- Threat of new entrants
- Bargaining power of suppliers
- Bargaining power of buyers
- Threat of substitutes
- Industry rivalry

### 3.3 Industry Playbook Integration

Industry-specific patterns, key ratios, and warning signs from `reports/` industry-outlook layer. These are living knowledge artifacts, updated as cross-case learning accumulates.

## 4. Management Assessment Framework

**Management must be assessed through DECISION HISTORY, not charisma.**

### 4.1 Management Claim Ledger

Track management's public statements and compare against outcomes:

| Period | Claim | Outcome | Variance |
|--------|-------|---------|----------|
| FY2023 Q1 | "Revenue growth 15% driven by new product" | Actual 12% | −3% |
| FY2023 Q2 | "Margins stable despite inflation" | Operating margin −120bps | −120bps |
| FY2024 | "ROIC will recover to 15% by FY2025" | ROIC 11% | −4% |

### 4.2 Capital Allocation Ledger

Track every major capital allocation decision over 10-15 years:

| Year | Decision | Amount | Outcome | Per-Share Impact |
|------|----------|--------|---------|-----------------|
| 2018 | Acquired Company X | $2B | Written down 40% within 3 years | −$0.50/shr |
| 2019 | Increased R&D 30% | $500M | New product line → 15% of FY2024 revenue | +$1.20/shr |
| 2020 | $5B buyback at $45 | $5B | Reduced shares 8%; price now $65 | +$2.10/shr |
| ... | | | | |

### 4.3 Required Management Assessments

| Assessment | What to Measure | How |
|------------|-----------------|-----|
| **Buyback / Issuance History** | Was buyback timing intelligent? Did they buy high and issue low? | Price at buyback vs current; dilution trend |
| **M&A History** | Was value created or destroyed? | Acquisition ROIC vs WACC; write-downs |
| **Reinvestment Decisions** | Did they reinvest in the core business wisely? | Incremental ROIC; R&D effectiveness |
| **Incentive Structure** | Are incentives aligned with long-term value creation? | Compensation structure; vesting schedules; performance metrics |
| **Per-Share Value Creation** | Did per-share intrinsic value grow? | Per-share earnings, book value, FCF trend over 10+ years |

### 4.4 Promise vs Outcome

For each material forward-looking statement by management:

- What was promised?
- When was it promised?
- What actually happened?
- What was the variance?
- Was the variance explainable (external shock) or a management issue?

### 4.5 Time Horizon

Prefer 10–15 years of management track record where available. Shorter records are acceptable for younger companies but must be explicitly noted as a limitation.
