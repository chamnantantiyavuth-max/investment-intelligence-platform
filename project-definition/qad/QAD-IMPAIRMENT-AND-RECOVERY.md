## 1. Purpose

Define the analytical framework for:

1. **Dislocation Reconstruction** — precisely identify what broke and why
2. **Impairment Diagnosis** — classify the nature and severity of impairment
3. **Recovery Modeling** — define the path back to normalized economics

These three analyses answer the central QAD question: *Is this a high-quality business whose temporary economic impairment is being priced as permanent?*

---

## 2. Dislocation Reconstruction

Reconstruction must identify the specific things that broke. Vague "macro headwinds" or "industry challenges" are insufficient.

### 2.1 What Broke?

Identify the specific performance variables that deteriorated:

| Variable | Question | Evidence Source |
|----------|----------|----------------|
| **Revenue** | Did revenue decline? By how much? Volume, price, or mix? | 10-K/Q segment data |
| **Volume** | Did unit volumes fall? In which segments? | Segment reporting, industry data |
| **Price** | Did pricing power weaken? Discounting? | ASP trends, segment pricing |
| **Mix** | Did product mix shift negatively? | Segment margin comparison |
| **Margin** | Which margins compressed? Gross? Operating? By segment? | P&L, segment reporting |
| **Market Share** | Did the company lose share? To whom? | Industry data, competitor filings |
| **Churn** | Did customer retention deteriorate? | Customer metrics (if disclosed) |
| **ROIC** | Did returns on capital decline? Why? | Capital allocation analysis |
| **Cash Conversion** | Did cash conversion worsen? Inventory? Receivables? Payables? | Cash flow statement, working capital |

### 2.2 Root Cause Analysis

For each broken variable, trace the root cause:

| Question | Analysis |
|----------|----------|
| **Cause** | What is the direct cause of deterioration? (cyclical, competitive, technological, regulatory, self-inflicted) |
| **Peer Test** | Are peers experiencing the same deterioration? If yes → industry-wide. If no → company-specific. |
| **Moat Test** | Does the dislocation attack the moat directly, or does the moat protect the company during the dislocation? |
| **Reversibility** | Can the deteriorated variables recover? What would need to happen? |
| **Balance-Sheet Runway** | Can the company survive the dislocation without permanent damage? |
| **External Evidence** | What external evidence confirms or contradicts the dislocation narrative? |
| **Price Test** | Has the stock price fallen MORE than the economic damage justifies? |
| **Thesis Killers** | What would prove this dislocation is permanent? |

### 2.3 Cause Classification

| Cause Type | Characteristics | Recovery Pattern |
|------------|-----------------|------------------|
| **Cyclical** | Industry-wide, demand-driven, temporary | Recovery with cycle upturn |
| **Competitive** | Share loss to competitor, pricing pressure | Depends on moat response |
| **Technological** | Disruption risk, technology shift | Potentially structural |
| **Regulatory** | Law, regulation, or legal risk change | Depends on permanence |
| **Company-Specific** | Strategic error, product failure, management failure | Depends on leadership response |
| **Macro/External** | War, pandemic, tariff, currency, interest rates | Recovery with macro normalization |
| **Mixed** | Multiple causes interacting | Requires decomposition |

## 3. Impairment Diagnosis

### 3.1 Impairment States (Canonical)

| State | Meaning | Implications |
|-------|---------|-------------|
| **TEMPORARY** | Evidence supports recovery to normalized economics within a reasonable timeframe | QAD thesis viable if price implies permanent damage |
| **MOSTLY_TEMPORARY** | Most impairment is temporary; some residual permanent damage (e.g., lost market share that won't fully return) | Partial recovery; QAD thesis may still work at the right price |
| **MIXED** | Genuine structural impairment in some areas, temporary in others; net direction ambiguous | Requires careful weighting; high uncertainty |
| **STRUCTURAL** | Evidence supports permanent damage to the business model or competitive position | QAD thesis unlikely; NOT_QAD_STRUCTURAL verdict |
| **UNRESOLVED** | Insufficient evidence to classify; material evidence gaps remain | Cannot reach verdict without further investigation |

### 3.2 Mandatory Output

Every impairment diagnosis must produce:

```text
Primary Diagnosis:  TEMPORARY (for example)
Strongest Competing Explanation: STRUCTURAL — [concise case]
Why Primary Currently Dominates: [evidence-based reasoning]
Weakest Link: [the most assailable part of the primary diagnosis]
Evidence That Would Flip Diagnosis: [specific, observable conditions]
```

This structure ensures the diagnosis is falsifiable and the uncertainty is explicit.

### 3.3 Impairment Dimensions

| Dimension | Assess | Evidence |
|-----------|--------|----------|
| **Revenue Impact** | Is revenue decline temporary or permanent? | Volume vs price vs mix; end-market demand |
| **Margin Compression** | Are margin pressures structural (competitive) or temporary (cost cycle)? | Input costs, pricing, competition |
| **Market Share** | Is share loss recoverable or structural? | Competitive dynamics, moat test |
| **Customer/Channel** | Are customer/channel losses recoverable? | Switching costs, contract status |
| **Capital/Investment** | Is the business under-investing due to distress? | CapEx vs depreciation, R&D trends |
| **Balance Sheet** | Is the balance sheet weakened permanently? | Leverage, liquidity, refinancing risk |
| **Talent/Organization** | Is talent leaving? Is organizational health suffering? | Employee metrics, Glassdoor, leadership stability |

## 4. Recovery Model

Every case with a TEMPORARY or MOSTLY_TEMPORARY diagnosis MUST define a recovery model.

### 4.1 Recovery Model Structure

```text
CAUSE → RECOVERY MECHANISM → LEADING EVIDENCE → EXPECTED SEQUENCE → TIME HORIZON → INVALIDATION
```

| Element | Definition | Example |
|---------|------------|---------|
| **Cause** | What broke? | Revenue declined 20% due to channel destocking |
| **Recovery Mechanism** | How does it recover? | Destocking ends → normal reorder cycle resumes |
| **Leading Evidence** | What early sign confirms recovery is occurring? | Distributor inventory weeks decline, order rates stabilize |
| **Expected Sequence** | In what order should recovery happen? | Orders stabilize → revenue stabilizes → margins normalize → ROIC recovers |
| **Time Horizon** | When should recovery be evident? | 2–4 quarters for consumption to normalize |
| **Invalidation** | What would prove the recovery thesis wrong? | If after 4 quarters orders haven't recovered instead of 2–4 |

### 4.2 Forbidden Reasoning

**Never use as causal reasoning:**
> *"It was historically a great company, therefore it will recover."*

Recovery must be supported by specific mechanism, not past reputation.

### 4.3 Recovery Scenarios

| Scenario | Description | Evidence Required |
|----------|-------------|-------------------|
| **V-Shaped** | Rapid recovery to normalized levels | External shock causing temporary disruption; no structural damage |
| **U-Shaped** | Gradual recovery over several quarters | Cyclical downturn; excess capacity needs to be worked through |
| **L-Shaped** | Permanent impairment to a lower baseline | Structural damage to revenue or margins; new normal is lower |
| **W-Shaped** | Double-dip: initial recovery, then renewed deterioration | Fragile recovery; second shock; uncertainty about stabilization |

### 4.4 Thesis Killers

For every recovery model, define the thesis killers — specific, observable events that would invalidate the thesis:

- Revenue fails to stabilize by [date]
- Key customer loss confirmed
- Technology disruption confirmed
- Regulatory action that changes business model
- Competitor achieves structural cost advantage
- Management actions that destroy value (dilutive acquisition, leveraged buyout)

## 5. Dislocation Signal Families (Discovery Input)

Dislocation Radar monitors these signal families:

| Family | Examples |
|--------|----------|
| **Price/Performance Dislocation** | Price decline vs market/peers, relative strength breakdown |
| **Multiple Compression** | P/E, EV/EBITDA, P/B compression vs history and peers |
| **Earnings Revision** | Negative guidance, Earnings Per Share surprise trend, consensus estimate trajectory |
| **Revenue/Volume/Mix Deterioration** | Organic growth deceleration, volume decline, negative mix shift |
| **Margin/FCF/ROIC Anomalies** | Margin compression, FCF negative, ROIC decline |
| **Working Capital Deterioration** | Inventory build, receivable aging, DSO expansion |
| **Management/Governance Events** | CEO departure, restatement, investigation, insider selling |
| **Regulation/Litigation** | Regulatory action, material litigation, recall, cyber incident |
| **Industry Demand/Capacity Shocks** | Destocking, capacity overbuild, price war, demand cliff |
| **Competitor Divergence** | Peer performance divergence, competitor gaining share |
| **Narrative Gap (FD #43)** | Market narrative implies permanent damage; evidence suggests temporary |

Reported business deterioration is NOT required — a material market dislocation with largely intact current economics may still create a QAD candidate.
