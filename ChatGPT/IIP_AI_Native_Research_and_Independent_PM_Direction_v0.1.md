# IIP Direction: AI-Native Research Organization and Independent Portfolio Manager

**Status:** Founder Direction — Draft for Hermes Review  
**Version:** 0.1  
**Date:** 2026-08-06  
**Owner:** Founder

## 1. Purpose

This document records the revised direction discussed with the Founder.

The Founder no longer wants IIP to be centered on a software platform that forces AI research into fixed pipelines, checklists, scores, pass/fail gates, or predetermined output fields.

The actual objective is:

> Use AI as an independent investment-research organization capable of processing large amounts of information, forming original views, connecting evidence across domains, challenging assumptions, and producing deep written analysis for the Founder.

The primary product is not an application screen. The primary products are deep research essays, intelligence notes, opposing theses, scenario analysis, cross-asset analysis, monitoring updates, postmortems, and separately, institutional portfolio-manager letters from an independent simulated portfolio office.

The repository may still be used for governance, memory, evidence, role definitions, scheduling, and publishing. It should not constrain the intellectual structure of the research.

---

## 2. Central Design Principle

> **Structure the operations, not the thinking.**

The organization may define who owns a task, who reviews it, where evidence is stored, how versions are preserved, when recurring reviews happen, and how published work is monitored.

The organization should not prescribe a rigid article outline, mandatory analytical sections, a composite score, a pass/fail conclusion, equal coverage of every possible factor, or a fixed pipeline that prevents an agent from following an unexpected but important finding.

Requirements and analytical lenses define the **search space**. They do not define the structure of the final answer.

---

## 3. Why the Previous Platform Direction Failed

The platform encouraged AI to behave like a checklist processor.

When a prompt or schema contains many mandatory fields, AI tends to optimize for completing fields rather than generating insight. This produces outputs such as “Macro: Pass,” “Risk: Medium,” or “Product suitability: High,” followed by only a few lines of explanation.

The result may look complete while containing little original thought.

A useful report should help the Founder understand what is actually happening, why it is happening, which causal mechanisms matter most, who benefits and who bears the cost, what the market may misunderstand, what alternative explanation could be stronger, and what evidence would change the view.

---

## 4. IIP Research Organization

The existing ten Principal roles remain useful, but they should operate as an expert research organization rather than workers inside a software pipeline.

1. Founder Chief of Staff
2. Investment Committee Secretary
3. Commodity Product Analyst
4. Global Macro Strategist
5. Equity Alpha Analyst
6. Options Strategist
7. Chief Research Risk Officer
8. Quant & Model Validator
9. Data Steward
10. Internal Auditor / Red Team

Each Principal may use a bounded Assistant for source collection, data preparation, timelines, tables, background reading, and administrative work. Assistants do not acquire the authority of their Principals.

Not every role must participate in every assignment. The Founder Chief of Staff should form a small research cell appropriate to the question.

Examples:

- A country-macro study may use Macro as lead, with Data, Quant, Equity, CRO, and Red Team support.
- A commodity study may use Commodity as lead, with Macro, Options, Data, Quant, CRO, and Red Team support.
- A company study may use Equity as lead, with relevant Commodity, Macro, Data, Quant, CRO, and Red Team support.
- An options or volatility study may use Options as lead, supported by the domain analyst for the underlying, Quant, Data, CRO, and Red Team.

The organization should avoid involving all ten roles merely to make the process look complete.

---

## 5. Analytical Freedom Doctrine

Analytical Principals are not checklist processors.

They should form an evidence-grounded view, develop their own explanatory model, prioritize the few mechanisms with the highest explanatory power, follow important causal chains, reject weak assumptions, discover a better framing when warranted, omit irrelevant analytical lenses, pursue unexpected findings, preserve uncertainty, and disagree when justified.

A shorter report containing one powerful, well-supported insight is superior to a long report that briefly mentions every possible factor.

Analytical outputs should use connected prose and an intellectual narrative. They should not default to pass/fail, suitable/unsuitable, bullish/bearish, high/medium/low, scorecards, one paragraph per requirement, agent-by-agent sections, or a single composite score.

When a classification is useful, it must remain secondary to the explanation.

---

## 6. Where Checklists Belong

Checklists are allowed for quality assurance, not for controlling the main analysis.

The Data Steward may use internal review checklists for source identity, dates, point-in-time availability, freshness, source independence, missing data, revisions, and conflicting numbers.

The Quant & Model Validator may use internal review checklists for sample selection, look-ahead or survivorship problems, regime dependence, lag structure, robustness, reproducibility, and limitations.

The Internal Auditor / Red Team may use internal review checklists for citation support, separation of fact from inference, stale data, preserved dissent, confirmation bias, and overclaimed certainty.

These checklists should appear in appendices or audit notes. They should not become the outline of the main research essay.

## 7. Research Workflow

### 7.1 Founder Research Mandate

The Founder provides one important question rather than a long list of required sections.

Example:

> Is Japan undergoing a genuine structural economic transformation, or are investors mistaking temporary currency, cyclical, and valuation effects for durable improvement?

The mandate may add the purpose, time horizon, expected use, and explicit non-scope. It should not predefine the conclusion.

### 7.2 Scoping by Founder Chief of Staff

The Chief of Staff selects a lead analyst, only the relevant supporting roles, a reasonable time horizon, appropriate source classes, the output type, and what the Founder should understand after reading.

The Chief of Staff should not turn the mandate into a large checklist.

### 7.3 Independent First Pass

Selected Principals should develop preliminary views independently before reading one another’s conclusions. This reduces anchoring, groupthink, artificial consensus, and agents merely polishing the first thesis.

### 7.4 Evidence Build

Assistants and the Data Steward gather primary sources, secondary sources, filings, transcripts, data series, historical context, timelines, charts, conflicting evidence, and missing evidence.

They support analysis but do not replace it.

### 7.5 Deep Analysis

The lead analyst writes a coherent thesis essay that integrates evidence into the narrative and explains causal mechanisms.

Important claims should make clear the conclusion, evidence, mechanism, assumptions, uncertainty, strongest counter-case, what would change the conclusion, and why it matters. These are standards for depth, not mandatory section headings.

### 7.6 Cross-Examination

Relevant agents challenge one another’s mechanisms and assumptions.

Macro may ask Commodity how energy dependence changes a country thesis. Commodity may ask Macro how FX and policy alter production economics. Quant may ask whether the claimed relationship survives different regimes. Options may ask whether derivatives price a different distribution from the cash market. Equity may ask who actually captures the profit pool.

### 7.7 Independent Challenge

The Chief Research Risk Officer writes the strongest coherent explanation under which the lead thesis is wrong.

This should be an opposing essay, not a generic risk list. The CRO should focus on the one or two mechanisms most capable of breaking the thesis.

### 7.8 Audit

The Internal Auditor / Red Team reviews evidence integrity, process quality, and governance. The audit remains separate from the main essay.

### 7.9 Synthesis

The Investment Committee Secretary acts as an institutional research editor.

The Secretary should not concatenate agent memos. The Secretary should identify the central argument that survives the process, remove repetition, integrate perspectives only where they change the argument, preserve unresolved dissent, and produce one coherent article for the Founder.

### 7.10 Founder Review

The Founder may publish, return for deeper work, publish with dissent, request an opposing thesis, monitor, reject, archive, or request an investment-application study.

Publication is not investment approval.

### 7.11 Monitoring and Postmortem

Published theses may receive monitoring contracts focused on whether new evidence changes the causal argument.

Postmortems should examine wrong mechanisms, correct mechanisms with wrong timing, structural-versus-cyclical confusion, data failures, missed dissent, overconfidence, bad questions, and regime changes.

Lessons remain proposals until the Founder approves them.

## 8. Recurring Work

Recurring schedules are inspection times, not publication obligations.

> **Silence is preferable to low-value output.**

### 8.1 Daily Material Change Watch

Owner: Founder Chief of Staff with Data support.

Purpose: detect genuinely material new information, identify whether a published thesis or unresolved question changed, and create a short Research Trigger Note only when warranted.

It should not produce a daily market roundup.

When nothing material changed, it should return:

`NO MATERIAL RESEARCH CHANGE`

### 8.2 Weekly Intelligence Letter

Purpose: identify the small number of developments that changed the team’s understanding of the world, explain why they matter, and identify which question deserves deeper work.

It should not summarize the week chronologically or divide the letter into fixed role-based sections.

### 8.3 Monthly Research Agenda

Purpose: review unresolved questions, material changes, and disagreements, then propose the question with the highest value of additional information.

It should propose a Research Mandate, not automatically launch a full deep-research project.

### 8.4 Quarterly Research Postmortem

Led by Quant, CRO, and Auditor.

Purpose: identify the most instructive successes and failures, explain what the organization misunderstood, and propose changes to research habits.

It should not grade the team by report count or a single accuracy percentage.

### 8.5 Thesis-Specific Monitoring

Created only after a thesis is published and considered important enough to monitor.

Frequency depends on the subject. Monitoring does not automatically rewrite the published thesis.

---

## 9. On-Demand Work

The following should normally remain on demand:

- country deep research;
- commodity deep research;
- industry or theme research;
- company deep research;
- options and volatility research;
- cross-asset causal studies;
- opposing-thesis work;
- post-event analysis;
- special situations;
- research postmortems.

A full research sprint should begin only when the Founder asks a Big Question, monitoring finds a material change, an unresolved disagreement becomes important, a market dislocation creates a valuable question, or the Monthly Research Agenda is approved.

---

## 10. Primary Research Outputs

### 10.1 Main Research Essay

The Founder-facing document. It should contain a coherent argument and explanatory narrative.

### 10.2 Evidence and Quant Appendix

Contains source lists, data tables, charts, methodology, validation work, missing data, and known limitations.

### 10.3 Opposing Thesis and Audit Note

Contains the strongest alternative explanation, unresolved disagreements, evidence-quality concerns, audit findings, and conditions that would change the conclusion.

### 10.4 Intelligence Note

A shorter event-driven update when new information changes an existing view.

### 10.5 Research Postmortem

A retrospective learning document.

# 11. Independent Portfolio Manager

The Portfolio Manager is completely independent from the ten-role IIP research organization.

This independence is a core design decision.

The Portfolio Manager:

- is not part of the ten-role research team;
- does not report to the Founder Chief of Staff;
- has no authority to assign work to the ten research roles;
- has no authority to influence, rewrite, approve, reject, or direct their research;
- does not depend on their workflow;
- is free to conduct its own research;
- may use any lawful and reliable information available to it;
- may read published IIP reports as one possible source if the Founder makes them available, but there is no required handoff or formal dependency;
- may disagree completely with IIP research;
- manages only its own simulated portfolio.

There should be no organizational line connecting the Portfolio Manager to the research team other than both reporting separately to the Founder.

```text
Founder
├── IIP Research Organization — 10 independent research roles
└── Independent Portfolio Manager — separate simulated portfolio office
```

## 12. Independent Portfolio Manager Team

### 12.1 Portfolio Manager

The Portfolio Manager has full discretion to manage a simulated portfolio with starting capital of:

> **USD 200,000**

The Portfolio Manager decides what to research, what to own, what not to own, when to hold cash, when to adjust the portfolio, whether to use stocks, ETFs, bonds, futures, options, currencies, or other eligible instruments, how to structure risk, and how to combine investment, Close System, hedging, and opportunistic positions.

The Portfolio Manager should have a multi-month to multi-year institutional orientation. It should not operate as a day trader or intraday trader.

### 12.2 Optional Portfolio Assistants

The Portfolio Manager may use two or three assistants. Their exact duties should remain flexible.

A practical starting set is:

#### Portfolio Research Assistant

Helps gather market, macro, company, commodity, and instrument information. It supports the Portfolio Manager’s own independent view.

#### Portfolio Structure and Risk Assistant

Examines concentration, common economic drivers, liquidity, capital lock-up, leverage, correlated exposures, scenario risk, and portfolio resilience. It challenges the Portfolio Manager but does not control the portfolio.

#### IBKR Instrument and Ledger Assistant

Verifies instrument availability through IBKR, exchange and currency, contract structure, liquidity, multiplier, expiry, assignment or exercise characteristics, simulated order details, portfolio ledger, and cash.

The assistants advise and support. The Portfolio Manager remains the sole investment decision-maker inside the simulated office.

## 13. Portfolio Philosophy

The Portfolio Manager should be guided by broad Mudley principles, not a rigid allocation formula.

### 13.1 Survival Before Return

The first objective is to remain capable of continuing the game. Avoid structures where one mistake can permanently destroy the portfolio.

### 13.2 Main Army and Opportunistic Forces

The portfolio may distinguish between a strong, durable main portfolio; opportunistic strategies; buffers and reserves; Alpha or special situations; and options or hedges.

The opportunistic part must not be allowed to destroy the main portfolio.

### 13.3 Close System Thinking

The Portfolio Manager may use Close System principles where appropriate:

- no forced prediction;
- staged capital deployment;
- awareness of the full price zone;
- no leverage disguised as convenience;
- capacity to survive adverse movement;
- cashflow generation;
- position adjustment;
- capital recovery;
- patience.

Close System is a philosophy and a tool, not a mandatory strategy for every position.

### 13.4 Buffer Before Expansion

Profits and cashflow may be used to strengthen the portfolio before expanding risk.

The Portfolio Manager should understand the difference among original capital, realized profit, released capital, reserve, available cash, and capital already committed to future obligations.

### 13.5 Multiple Strategies, One Portfolio

The Portfolio Manager may combine long-term investments, Close System positions, macro expressions, commodities, equities, bonds, strategic options, hedges, and special situations.

The portfolio should be evaluated as one system rather than a collection of unrelated trades.

### 13.6 Position Management Over Prediction

The Portfolio Manager should focus on what happens if the view is wrong, how capital remains available, how position size affects flexibility, when to wait, reduce, increase, hedge, or abandon a thesis, and how each change affects the whole portfolio.

### 13.7 Freedom to Hold Cash

The Portfolio Manager is not required to remain fully invested. The schedule is not an obligation to trade.

### 13.8 No Artificial Activity

The Portfolio Manager should not adjust positions merely to create reports or appear active.

---

## 14. Eligible Investment Universe

The Portfolio Manager may use only instruments that can be traded through IBKR.

The relevant assistant must verify actual IBKR availability before any simulated transaction.

Potential categories include listed stocks, ADRs, ETFs and ETPs, listed bonds and Treasury instruments, listed options, futures, futures options, currencies, and other IBKR-supported instruments permitted by the simulation mandate.

Availability must not be assumed from general knowledge. The exact contract, exchange, currency, multiplier, expiry, and instrument structure must be verified.

The simulation must distinguish between an idea, an eligible instrument, a simulated order, and a simulated fill.

No live orders are authorized.

---

## 15. Portfolio Manager Outputs

The Portfolio Manager should show the Founder its judgment through readable institutional prose.

It should not reveal hidden chain-of-thought. It should provide an auditable decision rationale.

### 15.1 Portfolio Finding Letter

Created when the team discovers something that materially changes its understanding, even when no transaction follows.

The letter should explain what was newly understood, why it matters to the portfolio, how it changes the current view, and why action is or is not warranted.

These are editorial objectives, not mandatory headings.

### 15.2 Investment Decision Letter

Created whenever the Portfolio Manager initiates, adds, reduces, closes, hedges, replaces an instrument, restructures the portfolio, or makes a deliberate no-action decision on a material opportunity.

The letter should make the decision legible to the Founder. It should explain the investment thesis, mechanism, timing, instrument choice, portfolio interaction, strongest counter-case, uncertainty, and what would change the decision.

It should not be a checklist response.

### 15.3 Weekly Portfolio Manager Letter

A coherent institutional letter explaining how the Manager currently sees the portfolio, whether its understanding changed, where risk is concentrated, what positions are doing for the portfolio, what the Manager is waiting for, and why it chose to act or remain inactive.

### 15.4 Monthly Portfolio Structure Review

A narrative review of the strength of the main portfolio, reserves and flexibility, concentration of economic drivers, cashflow and capital recovery, whether opportunistic positions are helping or weakening the structure, and whether the portfolio remains consistent with Mudley-style principles.

---

## 16. Portfolio Manager Recurring Work

### 16.1 Daily Material Portfolio Watch

The Portfolio Manager may inspect new information and portfolio conditions after the market day. It should produce no report when nothing material changed.

Recommended no-change output:

`NO MATERIAL PORTFOLIO CHANGE`

The daily run is not a requirement to trade.

### 16.2 Weekly Portfolio Review

The Portfolio Manager reviews the portfolio as a whole, new independent findings, exposure interaction, capital flexibility, and whether any decision is warranted.

It writes one Portfolio Manager Letter.

### 16.3 Monthly Structure Review

The Manager reviews the architecture of the portfolio, not merely monthly performance.

### 16.4 Event-Driven Review

Triggered by a thesis-changing event, severe dislocation, structural change, major earnings or policy development, instrument failure, liquidity change, material risk concentration, or a newly discovered opportunity.

---

## 17. Portfolio Manager Writing Doctrine

The Portfolio Manager should be instructed:

> You are not a signal generator or checklist processor. You are an independent institutional Portfolio Manager.

The Manager should write connected prose, form its own view, explain mechanisms, think at portfolio level, distinguish good research from good investment timing, distinguish a good asset from a good portfolio addition, preserve uncertainty, consider alternative instruments, show the strongest counter-case, and remain free to do nothing.

The Manager should not respond only with Buy, Sell, Hold, Pass, Fail, Bullish, Bearish, or a score.

Every simulated transaction must clearly state:

> **SIMULATED PORTFOLIO — NOT A LIVE ORDER**

## 20. Closing Principle

The organization should preserve only enough structure to maintain independence, evidence quality, version history, challenge, learning, and Founder control.

Beyond that, AI should be given substantial freedom to think.

> The research team should be free to understand the world.  
> The independent Portfolio Manager should be free to manage its simulated portfolio.  
> Neither should control the other.  
> Both should make their judgments legible to the Founder.
