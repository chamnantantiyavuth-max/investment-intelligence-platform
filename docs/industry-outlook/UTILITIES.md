## Supply Chain

Once "widow-and-orphan" monopolies; now unbundled into four segments as deregulation proceeds:

| Segment | Role | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Generators** | Create electrical power | Established utilities + merchant generators (speculative capacity) |
| **Network Operators** | Grid/regional/distribution networks | Natural monopolies — duplicate investment redundant; heavily regulated |
| **Energy Traders & Marketers** | Futures/derivatives, structured products; stable-price supply | Highest-risk segment — instant reaction to wholesale swings |
| **Retail Service Providers** | Sell to end users (often bundled gas/water/financial services) | Consumer choice in most US states |

## Analyst Insight

- **Safe-haven image faded:** open markets divert cash to expansion; more risk, more opportunity
- **Merchant/wholesale traders = highest risk** — shares react instantly to wholesale price swings, credit ratings, headlines
- Risk-averse play = **regulated transmission/distribution** — buffered from commodity swings, modest returns
- **Dividends still matter** — utilities maintain cash distribution; dividend yield is the income gauge
- Corporate reputation matters — constant regulator/environmentalist/public scrutiny
- Wholesale deregulation (US 1996) + state-by-state liberalization continue; politics around retail pricing persist

## Porter's 5 Forces

1. **New entrants:** HIGH barriers — plant fixed costs, regulatory approval, brand trust, unit-cost scale; unbundling opens trading/retail entry
2. **Suppliers:** significant — power systems supply dominated by small handful; vertical unbundling dilutes individual leverage
3. **Buyers:** power shifting to buyers — electricity is a commodity; commercial/industrial leverage via PPAs; consumer buying groups/co-ops
4. **Substitutes:** power has NO substitute (inelastic short-term demand) — but distributed generation (microturbines, fuel cells) threatens grid bypass over time
5. **Rivalry:** increasingly fierce — fight for market share/economies of scale; commoditization drives profitability down
