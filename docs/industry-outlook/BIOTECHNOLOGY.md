# Industry Outlook — Biotechnology

> Source: Investopedia Industry Handbook: Biotechnology (2015) — `docs/Books/The Industry Handbook_ Biotechnology.pdf`
> ⚠️ FD #58: all figures below are **2015 point-in-time** and must be re-verified before use. No Direct Commodity Investment section — biotech has no directly-traded underlying commodity (README commodity-only rule).

## Supply Chain

Biotech uses biological processes for products/solutions (DNA discovery 1953 → patentable life forms (1980s US Supreme Court) → modern industry). Value chain is **IP-driven** — patents define the industry.

| Stage | Role | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Research/Development** | Multi-year, high R&D cost, little revenue | Partnership with larger firms is the norm to fund development |
| **Clinical Trials** | FDA approval pathway | Phase I → II → III (see below) |
| **Marketing/Distribution** | Small firms lack distribution → license to partners | Marketing alliances often problematic |
| **Manufacturing** | Production at scale | Capital-intensive |

**Common applications:** agriculture (improved foods, pest control), industry (detergents, chemicals, textiles, waste reduction), health care (drugs, vaccines, gene therapy, tissue), research (genome, disease detection).

## What to Measure (Key Ratios/Terms)

| Term | What it signals | Where to find it |
|---|---|---|
| **R&D as % of Sales** | Higher = more new-product development; compare within industry only | Income statement |
| **Cash position / Current ratio** | Survival — cash-stricken biotech dies; equity financing dominant (banks refuse high-risk, no collateral) | Balance sheet |
| **Pipeline breadth** | Several products in development = one failure doesn't kill the company; too many = loss of focus | Company disclosures |
| **FDA approval stage** | Closeness to revenue; one year's difference = millions of dollars | FDA tracking, company filings |
| **Market size of target disease** | Treatment drugs > vaccines financially (continuous vs one-time use) | Epidemiology, industry research |
| **Orphan drug status** | Rare disease (<200K patients) — small demand but government-subsidized costs | FDA orphan drug list |

**FDA approval process [2015]** ⚠️ TODO-UPDATE (verify current trial norms):
- Phase I (~1 yr): 20–80 healthy individuals — dosage + safety
- Phase II (1–3 yrs): 100–300 patients — effectiveness + side effects
- Phase III (2–3 yrs): 1,000–5,000 patients — long-term side effects, placebo-controlled
- **~1–2 of every 20 drugs entering trials gain final approval [2015]**

## Where to Look

- FDA website — approvals, clinical guidance
- BioSpace — regulation news, industry developments
- Company balance sheets — cash runway (current ratio)
- Pipeline disclosures — clinical stage per product
- **Generic drug threat:** post-patent copycats sell at a fraction of cost — watch IP cliffs

## Analyst Insight

- **Not screenable by earnings/revenue alone** — need market potential of each drug + approval probability
- Analyze product portfolio: sales trends (existing products) + pipeline (disease target, market size)
- **Cash runway is king:** where money comes from (equity vs debt) and where it goes (R&D vs discipline)
- Financing environment matters: low rates / receptive IPO market → lower entry barriers
- Simple stock screens miss the diamonds — requires understanding the specific biotech sub-area

## Porter's 5 Forces

1. **New entrants:** HIGH funding barrier (R&D budgets); specialization is also a barrier (few experts in obscure fields)
2. **Suppliers:** weak overall — value is IP, not materials; but small firms must license distribution to suppliers
3. **Buyers:** varies — consumer drugs (weak buyer power) vs government/hospital sales (strong buyer power)
4. **Substitutes:** patent protection is temporary — generics + foreign IP infringement undercut price; high in practice
5. **Rivalry:** EXTREMELY intense — 1,000+ North American companies [2015], top 1% hold majority of revenue; trade secrets valuable
