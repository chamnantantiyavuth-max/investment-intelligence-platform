## Supply Chain

The 2015 handbook focuses on **web portals** (AOL, Yahoo!, AltaVista → multi-purpose portals); the durable structure is the **audience → monetization** chain that still underpins internet platforms.

| Layer | Role | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Audience acquisition** | Free content/services to attract users | "Stickiness" = brand + market power |
| **Monetization** | Advertising (primary), paid content, eCommerce | Over-reliance on ads = volatile revenue |
| **Distribution partners** | Alliances for audience reach | Without them, survival is hard |
| **Infrastructure** | Servers, bandwidth, delivery | Costs scale with freeloaders |

**Durable lesson:** converting users into paying customers is the industry's eternal challenge — the ad model subsidizes free users; the cost of serving a vast free user base can eliminate margin gains.

## What to Measure (Key Ratios/Terms)

| Term | What it signals | Where to find it |
|---|---|---|
| **Click-Through Rate (CTR)** | Immediate ad response | Ad platforms |
| **Stickiness (time per user)** | Loyalty → brand/market power | Analytics |
| **Burn Rate** | Cash spend while waiting for profitability | Cash flow statement |
| **Alliances** | Distribution network strength | Company disclosures |
| **Price/Sales** | Preferred over P/E when earnings absent — revenue proxies marketplace acceptance | Market data |
| **EV/EBITDA** | Yardstick for capital-heavy tech; increasingly criticized | Market data + financials |

## Where to Look

- Company financials — revenue mix (ad vs subscription vs commerce), cash runway
- Nielsen/NetRatings (2015 link) — traffic rankings; modern equivalents: Similarweb, App Annie
- Advertising market trends — ad spend has large discretionary component, cyclical
- Industry news (Internet Week/Traffick [2015 links] — verify current sources)

## Analyst Insight

- **Biggest portals attract biggest ad revenue** — traffic concentration persists
- **Non-advertising internet players hold up better in down markets** than ad specialists — revenue diversification matters
- Comparative ratios (P/S, EV/EBITDA) are not straightforward: retail portals (Amazon) vs service portals (Yahoo) have structurally different cost bases — compare like-for-like
- **Valuation can be wiped out by sentiment** — quantitative measures carry less weight here; caution required
- The "X-rated" subindustry lesson [2015]: when users genuinely value content, they pay for it — willingness-to-pay exists (relevant to today's subscription shift)

## Porter's 5 Forces

1. **New entrants:** cost of entry has FALLEN fast (off-the-shelf systems, cheaper bandwidth, abundant developers) — but brand + promotion require deep pockets
2. **Suppliers:** portals own little — content supplied externally → supplier power grows; telecom bandwidth switching costs are high once hooked up
3. **Buyers:** strong — visitors one click from competitors; advertisers squeeze terms (portal reliance on ad dollars)
4. **Substitutes:** constant — other sites, original content suppliers, TV, magazines, directories; original content suppliers are a ready substitute set
5. **Rivalry:** fierce — feeble entry barriers + substitutes + buyer power drive profitability down
