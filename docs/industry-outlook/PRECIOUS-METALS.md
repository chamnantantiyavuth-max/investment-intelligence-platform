## Supply Chain

Three types of firms; the industry is **not vertically integrated** (miners ≠ refiners ≠ sellers):

| Segment | Role | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Exploration** | Proves gold exists in an area | Few assets beyond drilling rights + capital; junior-heavy, speculative |
| **Development** | Operates on proven deposits | Buys finds from explorers or develops own; a "proven" area is the differentiator |
| **Production** | Full-scale extraction | 100K to several million oz/yr; blue-chip dominated |

Silver nuance: usually discovered **as a byproduct of gold/lead/copper mining** — its supply is partially a function of other metals' economics.

| **Futures Sales by Producers** | Producer hedging ("locking in" price); rising futures selling → bearish near-term | Gold Survey producer sales stats |

## Analyst Insight

- First look: **historical price range** (price fluctuates minute-to-minute)
- Second: **cost of production** — the structural floor/operating leverage
- Demand is relatively stable; **supply shocks** move prices (soaring or doldrums)
- Cost drivers: **location** (political risk), **ore quality** (oz/ton; oxide > sulphide), **mine type** (underground > open-pit cost)
- Low price → low production → high-cost producer exits → supply tightens (the structural shutdown cycle)
- Demand split [2015]: jewelry dominant; electronics/collectibles small — **TODO-UPDATE** (industrial demand has grown; check current mix)

## Porter's 5 Forces

1. **New entrants:** HIGH capital barrier — financing before any production; favorable terms critical
2. **Suppliers:** weak — land is plentiful; constraint is **permits/regulatory approval**, not supply
3. **Buyers:** strong — commodity, undifferentiated product → buyers push for lower prices/terms
4. **Substitutes:** moderate — diamonds/silver/platinum are substitutes but gold's global-standard status (same value US ↔ anywhere) protects it
5. **Rivalry:** competition is for **land/reserves**, not price (price is market-set)

## Direct Commodity Investment

The commodity itself is a distinct investment layer from mining equities — **pure price exposure, no company risk** (no management, dilution, mine accidents, or cost overruns), but also **no operating leverage** (no margin expansion as price rises). Close System treats physical commodities as first-class P1 products (CLOSE-SYSTEM-PRODUCT-RADAR §2.1: "have intrinsic industrial or monetary utility — even at cyclical lows, physical goods retain some value").

| Instrument | What it is | Layer | Watch |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Physical bullion / coins** | Bars or coins held directly (99.5%+ purity standard [2015]) | Direct | Storage/insurance cost; **coin premium vs spot** — physical shortage signal |
| **Physical-backed ETFs** (GLD, SLV, IAU) | Paper share of vaulted metal | Direct | **Premium/discount to NAV**; expense ratio; creation/redemption mechanics |
| **Producer ETFs** (GDX, SIL, XME) | Basket of miners | Indirect (company layer) | Diversification; leverage to metal price; individual miner risk |
| **Futures** (COMEX gold/silver) | Price exposure via contract | Direct | **Backwardation vs contango** — the market's own supply/demand read; roll costs |
| **Options on ETFs/futures** | Leveraged/defined-risk exposure | Direct (derivative) | IV, term structure, expiry — see Close System Options Overlay (deferred) |

**Signals that matter for the direct layer (Close System P2):**
- **Production Cost Proximity** — commodity price within 10–15% of marginal producer's all-in sustaining cost (AISC) → miners losing money, supply will contract
- **Dislocation** — spot gold premium over futures = physical shortage, not paper weakness (the exact "high accumulation for gold coin" reading)
- **Futures sales by producers** rising = producers locking in price = bearish near-term signal (handbook, durable framework)
- **Scrap recovery share** — non-mine supply elasticity; when high, price upside is capped [2015: >15% of world supply] ⚠️ TODO-UPDATE
