## Supply Chain

Four main product categories + an increasingly specialized structure:

| Segment | What it is | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Memory** | Temporary data storehouses | Consolidated — few giants survive (Toshiba, Samsung, NEC [2015]) ⚠️ TODO-UPDATE (leaders changed) |
| **Microprocessors** | CPUs with core logic | Intel-dominated; AMD the main challenger [2015] |
| **Commodity IC ("standard chips")** | Huge-batch routine processing | Asian manufacturers; razor-thin margins |
| **Complex SoC** | Full system on one chip | Highest opportunity; consumer feature-consolidation demand |

**Structure shift (durable concept):** traditionally companies controlled design→manufacture end-to-end; now **foundries** (manufacturing-only), **fabless designers** (design/marketing, no fab), and **specialized testers** form a specialized chain.

## What Drives Fundamentals

| Driver | Impact | Measured by |
|---|---|---|
| Market share gains | Revenue + earnings | Units shipped vs competition |
| Higher margins/profits | Absorption of fixed costs → lower unit cost | Process efficiencies |
| Higher product performance | Enthusiasm for end products | Benchmark results |

**Risks:** weak economy → shipment volume hit; delayed delivery → revenue/competitive loss; severe price competition → margin squeeze; failure to keep up with technology → cost control loss; slower computer replacement → depressed industry growth.

## Analyst Insight

- **Highly cyclical** — constant booms/busts; demand tracks end-market demand (PCs, phones, electronics [2015] → now: **AI compute, data centers, automotive — TODO-UPDATE**)
- Cyclicality is *identifiable*: market will turn; segment peaks/bottoms **stagger** (foundries bottom before designers)
- **Long lead times** — chip/fab development takes years; high-stakes, delayed payoff
- Technology vs market causality: **both directions valid** — recognize the interplay
- Fragmented end markets (PCs, comms infrastructure, automotive, consumer) mean excess capacity in one area rarely sinks the whole industry

## Porter's 5 Forces

1. **New entrants:** historically easy (design engineers spin out); now **billions for a fab** — high barrier; fabless model + alliances partially erode it
2. **Suppliers:** weak for large players (hundreds of suppliers = diffused power); **strong for merchant foundries** — cutting-edge equipment/skill concentration
3. **Buyers:** weak — key segments dominated by few large players
4. **Substitutes:** IP protection is temporary; **copy-cat reverse engineering** commoditizes fast — substitute threat is real within short windows
5. **Rivalry:** intense across the whole chain (makers, foundries, designers, distributors) — constant pressure for faster/cheaper/better
