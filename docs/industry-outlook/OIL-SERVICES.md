# Industry Outlook — Oil Services

> Source: Investopedia Industry Handbook: The Oil Services Industry (2015) — `docs/Books/The Industry Handbook_ The Oil Services Industry.pdf`
> ⚠️ FD #58: all figures below are **2015 point-in-time** and must be re-verified before use.

## Supply Chain

Two major oil-industry sectors; this handbook focuses on **upstream** (extraction + refining) vs downstream (commercial/resale).

| Segment | Role | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Drilling** | Physically drills and pumps oil | Contractors hired by producers; highly skilled labor; rig types: land (5,000–30,000 ft [2015]), submersible, jack-ups, drill ships |
| **Oilfield Services** | Supports drilling setup/maintenance | Seismic testing, transport, directional services |
| **Refining** | Separates crude into finished products | ~90% fuels (gasoline, aviation, distillate, LPG, kerosene [2015]); petrochemicals feed chemical plants; concentrated, capital-heavy, slow/stable |
| **Integrated majors** | Full chain: production → refining → marketing | Dominate much of the industry |

**Contract structure (durable):** drillers charge **dayrates** (daily work rate, location/rig/market dependent) or **meterage/footage** (per depth — a gamble on the driller since deposit depth is unpredictable). Falling dayrates directly cut service revenues (costs are largely fixed).

| Term | What it signals | Where to find it |

| **BTUs** | Energy comparison across fuel types (price per BTU) | Energy stats |

## Where to Look

- US Energy Information Administration (EIA) — supply/demand stats, energy sources
- ODS-Petrodata / Rigzone — rig counts, dayrates
- Baker Hughes rig count — utilization proxy
- Company 10-Ks — debt, cash flow, contract backlog

## Analyst Insight

- **Top-down approach dominates:** Economics/Politics → Supply & Demand → Rig Utilization → Contracts → Financials
- Oil company performance correlates strongly with the **commodity price** — but price-trend analysis alone is insufficient
- **First-mover on capex cycles:** services/drilling firms feel spending changes first (before production/profit arrives); demand shocks lag
- Supply shocks (OPEC) move prices; day-to-day noise shouldn't drive decisions — long-term trends matter
- Refinery exit barriers are high: a non-operating refinery has no value-adding capability (20 years with no new US refineries before ~2010s)
- Specialized/obscure services (seismic, directional drilling) are more resilient to substitutes

## Porter's 5 Forces

1. **New entrants:** HIGH barriers — specialized equipment ($1M+ pumping trucks [2015]), skilled labor scarcity, deep pockets required
2. **Suppliers:** significant power — small handful of large equipment/capital providers dominate
3. **Buyers:** power shifting to buyers — commodity product, buyers seek lower prices/better terms
4. **Substitutes:** alternatives (coal, gas, solar, wind, hydro, nuclear) — but oil feeds plastics/materials too; specialized services withstand substitutes
5. **Rivalry:** intense; slow growth + high exit barriers (refineries) trap players; margin pressure from commoditization

## Direct Commodity Investment

Oil itself trades directly — a pure-price layer separate from services/equities:

| Instrument | What it is | Layer | Watch |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Physical crude** | Actual barrels (storage/transport heavy) | Direct | Contango storage economics; quality benchmarks (WTI vs Brent) |
| **Oil ETFs** (USO, XLE) | Futures-based or equity basket | Direct/Indirect | **Futures roll decay** in contango — USO tracks futures, not spot; XLE = equities |
| **Crude futures** (WTI, Brent) | Contract price exposure | Direct | **Backwardation vs contango** — the market's own supply/demand read |
| **Options on futures** | Defined-risk exposure | Direct (derivative) | IV, term structure, expiry — Close System Options Overlay (deferred) |

**Signals that matter (Close System P2):** backwardation = current tightness (bullish); steep contango = oversupply, storage economics matter; OPEC quota decisions as scheduled supply shocks; rig count as activity leading indicator (falling rigs → future supply contraction).
