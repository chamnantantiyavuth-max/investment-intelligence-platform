# Industry Outlook — Telecommunications

> Source: Investopedia Industry Handbook: The Telecommunications Industry (2015) — `docs/Books/The Industry Handbook_ The Telecommunications Industry.pdf`
> ⚠️ FD #58: figures below are **2015 point-in-time**. No Direct Commodity Investment section — no directly-traded underlying commodity (README commodity-only rule).

## Supply Chain

Telecom = "the world's biggest machine" — networks connecting voice, data, and mobile. Structure shifted from national/regional monopolies to deregulated competition; mobile outpaces fixed-line; internet replaces voice as staple.

| Segment | Role | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Fixed-line / Broadband** | Voice + high-speed data (DSL era [2015]) | Residential competition brutal — price-led |
| **Mobile/Wireless** | Fastest growth historically | Spectrum is finite — license = entry barrier |
| **Corporate/Enterprise** | Premium services (private networks, videoconferencing) | Less price-sensitive; quality/reliability-focused |
| **Wholesale/Interconnect** | Network connectivity to other carriers + heavy users (ISPs, corporates) | Favors far-reaching network owners |

## What to Measure (Key Ratios/Terms)

| Term | What it signals | Where to find it |
|---|---|---|
| **EBITDA** | Baseline operating profitability — strips interest/taxes/capex (heavy capex = reported losses) | Income statement |
| **Churn Rate** | Customer retention — highest of any industry [2015]; brand + service quality mitigate | Subscriber reports |
| **ARPU** | Growth measure per subscriber; hard to sustain under competition | Subscriber reports |
| **Price/Sales** | Valuation when earnings absent; lower = better | Market data |
| **Net Debt/EBITDA** | Leverage comfort; credit agencies weight heavily; lower = better | Balance sheet |
| **Cash Flow** | Real money flow vs EBITDA illusion — profits can rise while cash ebbs | Cash flow statement |

## Where to Look

- FCC — regulation, spectrum, licensing
- Company subscriber/ARPU disclosures + capex guidance
- Credit rating agency reports (Moody's/S&P) — net debt/EBITDA thresholds
- Industry press (Telecommunications magazine [2015] — verify current)

## Analyst Insight

- **Size matters:** expensive business; contenders must be large with sufficient cash flow to absorb network costs; local-network owners (into homes/businesses) less reliant on interconnection
- **Earnings are tricky:** many companies have little/no earnings → P/S and EBITDA replace P/E
- EBITDA shows baseline profitability; **cash flow shows survival** — a company can record rising profits while cash flow ebbs (watch loan repayment + working capital)
- **Transmission systems obsolete quickly** — replacement cycles as short as every 2 years [2015]
- Net debt/EBITDA is the credit lens; debt is structural (capex financing) not optional

## Porter's 5 Forces

1. **New entrants:** HIGH barrier — access to finance + licenses (FCC approval) + finite good spectrum + scarce management talent
2. **Suppliers:** diluted — many equipment vendors; but scarce engineers/managers weaken operators in hiring
3. **Buyers:** rising power — services commoditized; residential switching costs low, enterprise customized-switching higher
4. **Substitutes:** serious — cable/satellite broadband, railways/utilities laying fiber, **internet telephony (VoIP)** eating core voice revenue
5. **Rivalry:** cut-throat — deregulation + new entrants + substitutes drive profitability down; high exit barriers (specialized equipment, swift obsolescence)
