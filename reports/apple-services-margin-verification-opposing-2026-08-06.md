> **Cover note (companion to `apple-services-margin-verification-2026-08-06.md`):** This is the CRO's preserved opposing essay for ORG-2026-0010 (RADAR-001 round 2), published as dissent per the research audit. (1) The essay supplied the opposing evidentiary case: it challenged the scope of what the new disclosure can clear. (2) The corrected verification note adopted the two cross-exam corrections (chronology-neutral reconciliation wording; "broadly stable" instead of "flattens") and the audit's seven corrections — the essay's central evidentiary boundaries are reflected in the note. (3) Publication preserves the dissent; it does not represent current agreement with every pre-correction characterization in the note. (4) The dissent's "four-basis-point" wording uses the displayed rounded endpoints (75.62% − 75.58%); the raw-input year-over-year movement is approximately 4.53 basis points — a rounding distinction, not a material contradiction. The essay is advisory research only — portfolio-blind, no price target, no buy/sell, no allocation advice. It is not retracted, resolved, or superseded by the base-case note, and it does not convert either interpretation into portfolio advice. The two documents are companions: this dissent is not abridged or silently harmonized with the base case.

The verification note reaches beyond what the new disclosure can support. Apple's Q3 FY2026 Services revenue and cost lines permit a clean reconstruction of a 75.6205% reported gross margin, and the corresponding Q3 FY2025 lines permit 75.5752%. That is useful evidence: it confirms the two quarterly calculations, their rounding, and their consistency with Apple's reported totals. But a point-for-point match across two Q3 observations is not a validation of the moat report's FY2023-to-FY2025 multi-year derived trend. The periods differ, the annual series and quarterly comparison may reflect different mixes and timing effects, and the derivation methodology has not been re-performed for every annual observation from primary-source inputs. Verification must attach to the exact proposition tested. Here, the tested proposition is narrow — two Q3 arithmetic results — not the broader shape, continuity, or comparability of a three-year trend.

The same distinction defeats the claim that the CRO dissent's "flattens at 75.6%" thesis is now source-cleared. A gross-margin level and a flattening inference are separate claims. The disclosed lines can source-clear that reported Q3 Services gross margin was approximately 75.6%; they cannot, by themselves, establish that margin has entered a plateau. Indeed, 75.62% versus 75.58% is merely a four-basis-point year-over-year difference within one seasonal quarter. It is consistent with flattening, but also with temporary mix effects, accounting timing, or offsetting movements beneath the aggregate. The dissent's actual flattening evidence depended on the FY2025 10-K-derived annual margin of roughly 75.4% in relation to prior fiscal years. Unless that annual series is independently reconstructed under one consistent methodology, the note has cleared the noun — the margin — not the verb — the flattening.

The tariff refund makes the over-clearance more consequential. The note acknowledges that segment allocation is unknown and that a tariff-normalized Services margin cannot be calculated, but then treats 75.62% as authoritative on a reported basis. That formulation understates the risk. Reported-basis arithmetic can be exact while the economic signal is distorted. If any material portion of the refund reduced Services cost of sales in Q3, then 75.62% embeds a one-time benefit and overstates recurring Services gross margin. Because Services cost of sales is only a fraction of Services revenue, even a refund that is modest at the consolidated level could produce a meaningful movement in segment margin if concentrated there. Unknown allocation therefore is not a peripheral caveat to an otherwise verified economic claim; it is an unresolved variable inside the numerator of the claimed margin itself. Until allocation is disclosed or bounded, the quarter cannot reliably anchor a structural margin conclusion.

Finally, wording such as "successfully verified ex post" and "strengthens confidence in the prior arithmetic" creates a provenance hazard. Readers and downstream systems may collapse four distinct statuses — primary-source reported inputs, recomputed quarterly ratios, an independently derived annual series, and an interpretive trend thesis — into one label: verified. That is precisely how derived evidence gets laundered into source-verified fact. No numerical correction may be required for the two Q3 calculations, yet an evidentiary correction is required: the verification perimeter must be explicitly confined to those calculations. The FY2023-to-FY2025 series remains independently derived until each annual data point, classification choice, and comparability assumption is re-performed and documented; the flattening thesis remains an interpretation; and the recurring economics of Q3 FY2026 remain unresolved while the tariff refund's segment effect is unknown.

A rigorous verdict would therefore read: "Q3 FY2025 and Q3 FY2026 Services gross-margin arithmetic is reproduced from disclosed revenue and cost lines on an unadjusted reported basis; this does not validate the moat report's annual trend, prove flattening, or establish tariff-normalized recurring margin." Anything broader over-clears the evidence and converts a narrow arithmetic confirmation into false assurance about trend and economics.
