# London Silver Vaults: First Watch-Item Test

**Thesis.** The June 2026 London vault reading materially weakens the published silver challenge memo's inventory-and-liquidity hypothesis on its first explicit watch item, as far as the visible-inventory proxy can take it. LBMA-reported silver holdings reached 902.843 million troy ounces — the highest observation in the supplied May 2025–June 2026 LBMA extract — up 137.954 Moz, or 18.04%, from June 2025. That is not the persistent decline in London stocks contemplated by the memo's confirmation case; it is a substantial visible-stock rebuild. The rebuild materially weakens the visible-inventory proxy for depletion — it does not directly measure the memo's "available stocks" watch item, which remains unresolved; this is material adverse evidence, not a completed partial break of that watch item. The appropriate conclusion is narrower than declaring the physical-deficit thesis invalid. Reported vault holdings are not equivalent to immediately available, unencumbered metal, and the broader break condition also requires normalization in lease rates and physical premiums, alongside insufficient investment absorption. Nevertheless, the burden of proof has shifted in one respect: an interpretation centered on depletion of London's reported metal can no longer rest on the aggregate vault series alone.

## The observation, quantified

The scale and timing of the rebuild are economically significant. London silver holdings increased from 764.889 Moz in June 2025 to 902.843 Moz in June 2026, a gain of approximately 138 Moz. That increase is more than three times the reported 40.3 Moz silver-market deficit for 2025 (137.954/40.3 = 3.42×) and exceeds the combined 2025 and forecast 2026 deficits of 86.6 Moz (137.954/86.6 = 1.59×). June's reported stock of 902.843 Moz was approximately 10.43 times the combined 86.6 Moz deficits — a stock-to-annual-flow comparison, not a measure of the rebuild itself. Holdings also advanced by 15.1 Moz in June alone (902.843 − 887.725 = 15.118) to establish the extract high, following a comparatively stable March-to-May interval (range 5.070 Moz).

These comparisons do not prove that the accounting deficits are misstated: a market deficit is a flow balance and can be financed through inventory mobilization, whereas the London series records metal held in one visible location. They do show that persistent deficits have coexisted with a large increase in reported London stocks rather than an observable drawdown there. With forecast mine output broadly flat at 844.1 Moz in 2026 (source-reported −0.3%, not independently derived in this artifact — the prior-year denominator is not in this register), the London inflow is consistent with one or more of inventory relocation, mobilization from previously unreported stocks, investment-product flows, imports, or changes in custody and ownership — the data rule against a contemporaneous surge in mine supply more strongly than they identify the actual source of the inflow.

## The squeeze footprint

The monthly sequence provides a clear quantitative footprint for the World Silver Survey 2026 statement that the October 2025 liquidity squeeze "subsequently eased as metal returned to London." LBMA holdings rose by 53.8 Moz in October, from 790.309 Moz to 844.105 Moz (53.796). A further 30.0 Moz arrived in November (29.961) and 20.3 Moz in December (20.291), taking year-end holdings to 894.357 Moz. The cumulative November–December increase of 50.3 Moz (50.252) equaled 93.4% of the October jump. This pattern is strongly consistent with the Survey's account: a sharp replenishment during the squeeze period was followed by continued rebuilding as dislocated metal moved into the London system. It should be treated as corroboration rather than a complete causal demonstration, because monthly vault totals do not identify the metal's origin, beneficial owner, encumbrance, or availability for lending. Even so, the timing and magnitude make the "metal returned to London" explanation more persuasive than an interpretation in which the squeeze eased despite continuing depletion of London's visible pool.

## The framework test

Against the published framework, the result is material adverse evidence against the visible-inventory proxy, not a completed break of the available-stock watch item. The memo stated that the inventory-and-liquidity interpretation would be confirmed if London available stocks and COMEX deliverable inventories declined persistently, lease rates remained elevated, and regional physical premiums recurred or persisted. The direction of reported aggregate holdings is opposite to visible-stock depletion; the direction of unencumbered or otherwise available stocks has not been established. This weakens the inventory-depletion limb of the reported-stock series and suggests that the 2025 squeeze may have reflected location, timing, ownership, or lendability constraints rather than an absolute shortage of silver.

However, "reported holdings" and "available stocks" are not interchangeable. Metal can be allocated, ETF-owned, operationally immobilized, or otherwise unavailable to the lease market. A larger gross stock can therefore coexist with tight marginal liquidity if only a limited portion is mobilizable on acceptable terms. The single SLV reading of 486.467 Moz as of 5 August 2026 underscores the potential importance of ownership structure, but without a comparable time series it cannot establish whether ETF flows supplied or absorbed the London rebuild.

## What would confirm or break the new reading

The next evidence must distinguish inventory abundance from liquidity abundance. Easing physical availability would be confirmed if London holdings remain elevated or continue to rise while silver lease rates normalize across tenors, COMEX registered or otherwise deliverable stocks rebuild, regional bar and coin premiums subside, and delivery conditions remain orderly. A flow decomposition showing metal arriving from non-London inventories, imports, recycling, or investment liquidation would further support relocation and mobilization as the mechanism financing the deficit. Conversely, the apparent easing would be broken if the headline vault total remains high but lease rates re-escalate, COMEX deliverable inventories fall persistently, regional premiums recur, or evidence shows that most incremental London metal is allocated or otherwise unavailable for lending and settlement. Renewed London drawdowns after June would also indicate that the rebuild was temporary rather than structural.

## The dissenting view

The Chief Risk Officer's strongest opposing essay is published as a companion: *The Rebuild Measures Gross Custody, Not the Free Float*. Its central argument: the 902.843 Moz reading measures gross metal stored in London — not the marginal ounces actually available to lease, sell, or deliver; the rebuild may be the deposit side of the same dislocation that produced the squeeze; and a deficit financed by inventory mobilization is the mechanism the inventory-liquidity thesis predicts, not its refutation. The audit-supported base case holds that the visible-inventory proxy is materially weakened while the available-stock watch item remains unresolved; the disagreement is real and preserved.

Limitations are material: no newly retrieved lease-rate curves, COMEX deliverable-stock data, regional premium series, or ETF-flow history; LBMA totals measure metal in vaults, not free float or lending availability; monthly observations cannot resolve intramonth stress. The analysis is advisory research only — portfolio-blind, no price target, no buy/sell, no allocation advice. On current evidence, the extract high clearly weakens the proposition that persistent deficits are depleting reported London inventories — the visible-inventory proxy — while leaving the broader liquidity question open pending lease-market, COMEX, premium, and ownership evidence.
