# The Case Against the Moat

**Companion to:** [Apple's Moat: How Durable, and What Would Break It](/library/apple-moat-2026-08-06) · Research mandate RM-2026-0001 · 3–5 year horizon · Advisory, not a recommendation

Apple's installed base may remain large and its earnings formidable, yet its moat can fail the durability test. The economically relevant moat is Apple's ability to convert ecosystem control into unusually high and growing rents. That control is concentrated in the iPhone acquisition funnel and the Services monetization layer. Over the next three to five years, regulation can weaken Apple's right to tax activity inside the ecosystem, AI can move discovery and transaction initiation above the operating system, China can reduce platform scale, and tariffs can compress the hardware economics left behind. No mass abandonment of iPhones is required. The moat can lose economic substance while the installed base continues setting records.

## The profit concentration is the starting point

In FY2025, Services produced $109.2 billion, or 26.2% of Apple's $416.2 billion revenue, but $82.3 billion of gross profit at a 75.4% gross margin — approximately 42.2% of Apple's $195.2 billion total gross profit (derived from disclosed figures; Apple FY2025 Form 10-K, fiscal year ended September 27, 2025, filed October 31, 2025, accession 0000320193-25-000079). Products earned a 36.8% gross margin. This is usually read as proof of a strengthening moat. The opposing interpretation: Apple depends increasingly on exceptionally profitable control points precisely when governments and new interfaces are attacking them. A modest decline in the economic yield on Services matters far more than its revenue share implies.

## Regulation is already separating customer attachment from monetization

To comply with the EU Digital Markets Act, Apple introduced alternative app distribution, alternative payment processing, and alternative fee structures. On April 23, 2025, the European Commission fined Apple €500 million and ordered it to remove restrictions preventing developers from steering users outside the App Store; Apple appealed. A US court order (2025 Injunction) prevents Apple from imposing any commission or fee on certain external purchases; the Ninth Circuit upheld it in part on December 11, 2025, and the Supreme Court granted certiorari on June 30, 2026 (Apple FY2025 10-K; Q3 FY2026 10-Q, accession 0000320193-26-000020, filed 2026-07-31). These precedents show that users can remain inside Apple's ecosystem while developers, payment providers, or customers retain economics Apple previously controlled. Remedies pioneered in one jurisdiction can become templates elsewhere, while developers route high-value transactions around Apple. The installed base need not decline for the platform's take rate to become harder to defend.

## AI can change where demand begins

Apple's switching costs were built in an app-centric era: users accumulated applications, credentials, media, files, and workflows while developers competed for discovery on Apple-controlled surfaces. An effective cross-platform agent can sit above those surfaces. If users ask an assistant to find, compare, purchase, schedule, or create, applications become execution engines and the assistant controls intent, recommendation, and routing. Apple may still provide the device, sensors, security, and operating system, but another layer can own the economically scarce moment — the decision. Apple cited an "all-new Siri AI" in the quarter ended June 27, 2026, but disclosed no usage, capability, retention, or commerce data (Apple Q3 FY2026 earnings release, furnished July 30, 2026, accession 0000320193-26-000018). The burden of proof lies with renewal, not incumbency: investment and integration do not establish that Apple will control the agent layer rather than supply premium hardware beneath it.

## The compound event is not required

Regulation and AI need not arrive as a dramatic compound event. Regulation lowers contractual and technical barriers to alternative distribution and payments; AI lowers the behavioral barrier by making those alternatives invisible to users. Together they can bypass the App Store without asking consumers to learn a new device or operating system. The ecosystem's strength could delay recognition: users stay, active devices grow, and Services revenue rises in nominal terms, while the marginal dollar of activity accrues to the assistant, model provider, merchant, or developer. Erosion would first appear as slower Services growth and margin compression, not installed-base collapse.

## The hardware foundation is less diversified than the narrative implies

iPhone generated $209.6 billion, 50.4% of FY2025 revenue — one category is both half of current sales and the principal gateway for future Services demand (Apple FY2025 10-K, accession 0000320193-25-000079). Apple warns that a decline in this category could significantly affect revenue and gross margins, and reports minority global share in smartphones, PCs, tablets, and wearables with competitors able to price at little or no profit. In an AI transition, rivals need not dislodge the whole installed base. Persistent leadership among premium users and developers in assistant quality, new form factors, cameras, or on-device models could weaken brand leadership first, product economics second, and the future Services funnel third.

## China can accelerate the process

Greater China contributed $64.4 billion, 15.5% of FY2025 revenue, down from 18.9% in FY2023; FY2025 weakness reflected lower iPhone sales (Apple FY2025 10-K, filed October 31, 2025, accession 0000320193-25-000079). The rebound to $18.8 billion, up 22.4% year over year in the quarter ended June 27, 2026, is counterevidence, as is the 21.7% rise in quarterly iPhone revenue to $54.3 billion (Apple Q3 FY2026 earnings release, furnished July 30, 2026, accession 0000320193-26-000018). But one strong cycle does not resolve a structural contest over local AI, data, distribution, and national preference. It proves Apple can still win a launch, not that it will remain the preferred gateway across several launches.

## The cost advantage is vulnerable just as Services rents come under pressure

Substantially all Apple hardware is made by outsourcing partners concentrated across China mainland, India, Japan, South Korea, Taiwan, and Vietnam; critical custom components can come from single or limited sources, and manufacturing purchase obligations were $56.2 billion as of September 27, 2025. Product gross margin slipped to 36.8% in FY2025 from 37.2% in FY2024, with tariff costs among the disclosed offsets (Apple FY2025 10-K, accession 0000320193-25-000079). In the quarter ended June 27, 2026, reported gross margin was 50.1%, but tariff refunds added approximately two percentage points, implying about 48.1% absent that benefit by simple subtraction (Apple Q3 FY2026 earnings release, furnished July 30, 2026, accession 0000320193-26-000018). Diversifying assembly cannot fully neutralize tariffs that reach the same Asian production hubs. Absorbing costs, raising prices, or pressuring suppliers consumes margin, elasticity, or resilience.

## The erosion would be easy to miss in per-share results

Apple repurchased $90.7 billion of shares in FY2025, equal to 81.4% of that year's $111.5 billion operating cash flow (derived: 90,711/111,482), and approximately $438.6 billion from FY2021 through FY2025; period-end shares fell 10.1% over those five fiscal years (Apple FY2025 10-K and SEC XBRL Company Facts, fiscal periods FY2021–FY2025, compiled August 6, 2026). It repurchased another $62.1 billion in the first nine months of FY2026, and in April 2026 announced an additional $100 billion program (Apple Q3 FY2026 10-Q, filed 2026-07-31, accession 0000320193-26-000020). A shrinking denominator can sustain EPS while Services yield weakens or product margins compress. The "buyback mask" is not that repurchases are illegitimate; it is that per-share resilience can outlast the organic economics funding it. Apple can keep reporting an all-time-high active-device base — as it did on June 27, 2026 — while profit captured per user begins to flatten.

## The case against durability is about control, not collapse

Initially, Apple can report record devices and cash flow while regulatory workarounds and third-party AI behavior remain too small or undisclosed to isolate. In years two and three, external payments and agent-led discovery can reduce incremental Services economics while premium competition and China make the iPhone funnel less reliable. By years four and five, lower Services yield and tariff-burdened product margins can compound into structurally lower organic profit growth even though customers still own multiple Apple devices. The brand survives, the ecosystem persists, and the moat nevertheless fails where it matters: protecting the duration and destination of economic rents.

---

**Single most important disagreement with the main essay.** The main essay treats economic hollowing as a compound break whose ingredients have not materialized; the opposing case holds that regulatory opening has already materialized, AI need only redirect marginal activity rather than replace iOS, and ordinary compounding is sufficient to erode the moat without a dramatic break.

**Earliest observable evidence that would prove this case right.** Apple continuing to report a record active-device base and resilient iPhone revenue while Services revenue growth decelerates materially and Services gross margin compresses for consecutive reporting periods — direct evidence that attachment persists while monetization weakens.
