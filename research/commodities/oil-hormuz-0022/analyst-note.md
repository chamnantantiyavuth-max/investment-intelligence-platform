## 1. Answer to the research question (headline)

**On the transmission (0012 leg):** the IEA quantification does NOT yet change the oil → inflation → real-yield transmission in *market pricing* — 10y breakevens are flat (T10YIE 2.26%, 8/12, vs 2.22% on 8/5), nominal 10y is flat (4.682, 8/12), and the real yield remains near cycle highs (DFII10 2.43%, 8/11). The dislocation raises the *risk* of the transmission firing — via product-driven core inflation (S6: diesel/jet export collapses, record Atlantic refining margins) — but as of the 13 Aug observation the inflation leg has NOT activated in prices. What changes is the *test vector for 0012*: the Hormuz dislocation is now a two-sided live experiment on gold's real-yield sensitivity (Section 4).

**On the demand-destruction repricing:** the 510 kb/d deepening is real and accelerating on the demand *side*, but the market has NOT yet repriced it as the leading edge of a demand-supply repricing — it has repriced the *supply* side. The WTI curve (S14) prices a sharp normalization into 2027 (front 82.90 → Dec-27 70.54, −14.9%) — exactly the IEA's own supply-recovery path (S4: +8.3 mb/d 2027 rebound). What is *not* priced is the demand-destruction leg: the market's backwardation embeds "deficit now, surplus late 2026" (S8), while the IEA's own demand cuts (S1/S2) and US distillate data (S16, −6.6% y/y) imply the rebalancing may arrive on the demand side faster than the supply-return path the curve assumes. That asymmetry is the live repricing risk.

Stocks are the swing variable, and they are drawing at 2.7 mb/d with onshore draws only recently slowing (S7 — the July draw was concentrated in oil-on-water, i.e., logistics, not demand).

### 2.2 What the market is pricing (S12–S14, fresh pulls 13 Aug 05:00 UTC)

- **WTI 82.93** (radar 82.90), **Brent 88.77** — +10.5% in 5 sessions (75.22 → 83.08).
- **Backwardation returned** (IEA S10 confirms; curve S14): front 82.90 → Dec-26 78.55 → Dec-27 70.54. The market prices **prompt physical tightness + a sharp 2027 normalization**.
- **Deferred curve ≈ IEA's 2027 supply story:** Dec-27 at 70.54 is the market's translation of +8.3 mb/d supply return. This is the supply leg, *priced*.
- **Products:** record Atlantic refining margins (S6) = product tightness priced in; US imports show the Gulf disruption (S18: Saudi crude to US −63% y/y, Iraq 0, Venezuela +743 as replacement barrel).

### 2.3 The reconciliation: what's priced vs what's not

| Leg | IEA-quantified | Market pricing | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Supply dislocation (Gulf shut-in, deficit) | −8.3 mb/d Gulf; 1.8 mb/d 3Q26 deficit | Backwardation; record margins; stocks draws visible | **PRICED** (prompt) |
| Supply normalization 2027 | +8.3 mb/d → 110.3 | Dec-27 70.54 (−14.9% from front) | **PRICED** (deferred) |
| Demand destruction deepening | −1.6 mb/d 2026; H2 −550 kb/d; distillate −6.6% y/y (US) | Backwardation assumes demand returns (+580 kb/d 4Q26, IEA S2 — market not testing this harder) | **NOT PRICED / UNDERTESTED** |
| Inflation transmission | product-driven (diesel/jet/freight), record margins | T10YIE flat 2.26%; 10y flat 4.682 | **NOT ACTIVATED** |
| Spare-capacity exhaustion risk | 1.09 mb/d buffer vs 1.8 deficit | No visible risk premium beyond prompt | **NOT PRICED** |

**The asymmetry that matters:** the market's curve is a supply-return curve. If demand destruction is instead the leading edge — the IEA cutting demand 510 kb/d deeper *this month*, H2 cuts of 550 kb/d, US distillate −6.6% — then the deficit closes *by demand collapse* faster than the +8.3 mb/d supply return, which is **bearish deferred** (the curve is already there) but **ambiguously bearish prompt**: demand destruction is the market's own equilibrating mechanism, and it argues the *peak* of the price spike is closer than the backwardation's steepness implies — OR that the physical market is tighter than the curve admits until the demand cuts actually bite. Both readings fail the same way if demand does NOT collapse as fast: prompt tightness persists, backwardation deepens, and the record-margin product market feeds inflation. That is the fork the falsification conditions (Section 5) monitor.

## 3. The transmission: oil → inflation → Fed/rate-path → real yields → gold (0012 linkage)

### 3.1 Oil → inflation: the channel is PRODUCT-driven, and it is live

The IEA's dislocation is not a generic crude spike — it is a **product-market dislocation**: diesel exports −1.3 mb/d y/y (~20% of seaborne trade), jet −670 kb/d (~34%), Atlantic refining margins at all-time highs (S6). Diesel, jet, and freight costs feed **core goods and services inflation** (transportation, logistics, airfares, industrial inputs) — the categories the Fed actually targets — not just headline energy. US data confirms the physical leg: distillate product supplied −6.6% y/y (S16) while crude imports shift to non-Gulf barrels (S18).

**Status: NOT yet visible in prices.** T10YIE 2.26% (8/12) is flat vs 2.22% (8/5) — the market has not moved inflation expectations on the dislocation. July CPI printed 3.4% y/y as expected (S19). The channel is armed, not fired.

### 3.2 Inflation → Fed/rate-path: the stagflationary bind

- July NFP **−23,000** (8 Aug) + July CPI **3.4%** (12 Aug) → September-hike odds **plunged** post-CPI (S19/S20; CME API 403, news-attested).
- The oil shock is **stagflationary by construction**: it raises headline/product inflation while the labor market is already softening. The Fed's reaction function faces a genuine two-sided risk — hike into an oil shock (2022 playbook, risks growth) vs hold through it (risks de-anchoring if product inflation persists). The market currently prices the dovish read (odds plunged); it is NOT pricing a Fed forced back to tightening by oil.

### 3.3 Fed/rate-path → real yields: the lever that matters for gold

Real yield = nominal − breakeven. With nominal 10y flat (4.682) and breakevens flat (2.26), **DFII10 2.43% (8/11) sits near cycle highs (peak 2.47%, 7/31)** (S15). The oil channel can move real yields two ways:
- **If breakevens rise with nominal anchored → real yields FALL** → the traditional gold channel turns *supportive* (the level finally comes down). This is the "transmission fires normally" outcome.
- **If the Fed is forced back toward tightening → nominal rises faster than breakevens → real yields RISE** → the clean 0012 diagnostic (gold's response to rising real yields) becomes live at scale.

Neither has happened as of 13 Aug — which is itself the finding: **the IEA dislocation has not yet altered the realized real-yield level that 0012 measures against.**

### 3.4 Real yields → gold: what this changes for ORG-2026-0012

0012's obs-2 ranking (S19): rate expectations = marginal driver; Hormuz geopolitical = active supporting bid; official-sector = unmeasured; dollar = mild. The 0012 conclusion: **rotation toward rate expectations observed; flow-dominance consistent, permanent break not established; the next diagnostic = gold's response to a rise in hike odds or realized real yields.**

The IEA dislocation sharpens that diagnostic into a two-sided test:
1. **Product-inflation leg fires** (T10YIE breaks above ~2.35–2.40 with nominal anchored) → real yields fall → gold rallies through the *traditional* channel → weakens the "break" case (channel works when it should).
2. **Fed is forced hawkish by oil** (hike odds re-price up) → real yields rise → **if gold still rallies, the break case strengthens; if gold falls, the traditional channel is intact.** This is the exact test 0012 said it needed and could not run (8/11–8/12 DFII10 unmeasured; CME 403).

So: the dislocation **does not change the transmission that 0012 is blocked on — it creates the conditions under which the blockage can be resolved.** The 0012 re-test window should be re-opened on the next inflation print (August CPI, mid-Sept) or any rate-odds move, with T10YIE/DFII10 as the primary instruments.

## 4. Is the 510 kb/d demand-destruction the leading edge of an unpriced repricing?

**Evidence FOR demand-destruction being the leading edge:**
- IEA cut 2026 demand 510 kb/d deeper *this month* and H2-26 by ~550 kb/d (S1/S2) — the cuts are accelerating, and they are explicitly attributed to "elevated fuel prices" + supply-chain disruption, not just war.
- US distillate product supplied −6.6% y/y (S16) — the industrial/freight leg is already contracting.
- Product markets are rationing by unavailability (diesel/jet export collapses, S6) — demand is being *forced down*, which is exactly how demand destruction presents before price elasticity shows up in consumer data.

**Evidence AGAINST (the IEA's own path says transitory):**
- The IEA forecasts demand **returning to +580 kb/d growth in 4Q26** and +2.4 mb/d in 2027 (S2) — the report itself does not extrapolate the destruction.
- US gasoline only −0.4% y/y and jet +8.4% y/y (S16) — consumer demand is resilient; the destruction is concentrated in freight/industrial (distillate), not households.
- The market's backwardation (S14) already embeds "deficit now, surplus late 2026" (S8) — the IEA's own balance says the repricing window is short.

**Verdict (draft):** the 510 kb/d deepening is **real, accelerating, and concentrated in the product/freight leg — but it is not yet the leading edge of a repricing the market has missed.** The market has priced the supply leg; it is *undertesting* the demand leg (the curve assumes demand returns). The falsifiable claim is not "demand destruction is happening" (it is) but **"demand destruction is the dominant equilibrator, arriving faster than the supply-return path"** — which would show up as: distillate/gasoline product-supplied y/y declines deepening while crude stays backwardated, and/or the IEA cutting 4Q26/2027 demand again in the September OMR. If instead product demand stabilizes, the supply-return path dominates and the Dec-27 70.54 level is fair.

## 6. Conclusion (draft)

1. **Supply side: priced.** The market's backwardation + record product margins are the IEA's dislocation, translated. The 2027 supply return (110.3 mb/d) is visibly priced in the deferred curve.
2. **Demand side: undertested, not unpriced.** The 510 kb/d deepening is real and concentrated in freight/industrial; the IEA's own path calls it transitory (4Q26 +580 kb/d). The market is not testing the demand-destruction-as-dominant-equilibrator scenario.
3. **Transmission: armed, not fired.** Oil→inflation is live through products (diesel/jet/freight) but invisible in breakevens (T10YIE 2.26%) and nominal yields (4.682). Real yields still near cycle highs (DFII10 2.43%).
4. **0012 impact: the dislocation does not change the transmission — it creates the two-sided test the watch-item needs.** Product-inflation firing → real yields down → traditional channel (break case weakens); Fed forced hawkish → real yields up → clean test (break case testable at scale). 0012's re-test window should be primed on F4 / rate-odds / DFII10 movement.
5. **The single most consequential asymmetry:** 1.8 mb/d 3Q26 deficit vs ~1.09 mb/d effective spare capacity, with stocks drawing 2.7 mb/d and onshore buffers only just slowing — any further Gulf escalation removes the last buffer, and the market is not pricing that tail.
