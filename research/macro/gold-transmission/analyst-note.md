# Gold vs Real Rates — Macro Strategist Note v2 (corrected)

**Card:** ORG-2026-0008 (RADAR-001) · **Status:** post-cross-exam (corrections applied) · **Author:** Macro Strategist (org-macro-strategist) · **Date:** 2026-08-06
**Evidence:** `research/macro/gold-transmission/evidence-log.md` · **Challenge:** `research/macro/gold-transmission/cross-examination.md`

---

The evidence supports a qualified, provisional reading: gold's observed resilience is consistent with temporary demand or risk-premium forces — conflict-, inflation-risk and policy-error hedging, with a possible official-sector component — outweighing the traditional real-yield drag in the current period. *(cross-exam correction 1: "overpowering" → "consistent with a flow-dominance hypothesis".)* The available register does not establish a permanent change in the transmission mechanism, nor does it identify which of those forces, if any, is responsible or how they rank. *(cross-exam corrections 1+6: no attribution, no ranking.)* The more defensible interpretation is regime dependence as the preferred provisional interpretation: real yields remain relevant, but their influence appears subordinated by demand for insurance against geopolitical disruption, persistent inflation and potential policy error. *(cross-exam correction: C1/C10 wording — "consistent with", not "supports".)*

The traditional channel is unambiguous. Gold produces no contractual income, so an increase in inflation-adjusted sovereign yields raises the opportunity cost of holding it relative to safe, interest-bearing assets, all else equal. *(cross-exam correction 2/C2: "all else equal" — a relationship, not a deterministic forecast.)* On 4 August 2026, the two-year Treasury yield stood at 4.20%, 73 basis points above its 2025 year-end level (radar-attributed comparator 3.47%); the ten-year nominal yield was 4.63%, up 45 basis points (comparator 4.18%); and the ten-year real yield was 2.40%, up 47 basis points (comparator 1.93%). *(cross-exam correction 3: year-end comparators labeled radar-attributed — not independently admitted in this register.)* The real-yield increase is the cleanest expression of the expected drag because it cannot be dismissed simply as compensation for contemporaneous inflation. All else equal, a 47-basis-point rise in the real return available on a long-duration Treasury inflation-protected security should have reduced the relative appeal of a non-yielding monetary asset.

Federal Reserve communication reinforces that restrictive signal in direction. *(cross-exam correction 8: "strengthens" → "directionally reinforces"; dissent preferences ≠ enacted policy.)* On 29 July, the FOMC held the federal funds target range at 3.50–3.75% by a 9–3 vote, with all three dissents preferring a 25-basis-point increase. The vote directionally reinforces the restrictive-policy and opportunity-cost signal, though a dissenting minority is not an enacted hike and does not by itself quantify the expected-rate-path effect or gold's response.

Observed performance nevertheless runs in the opposite direction. The LBMA PM gold price reached $4,206.60 per ounce on 5 August, 24.6% above its year-earlier level, implying a prior-year reference near $3,376.08 (derived: 4,206.60 ÷ 1.246). Meanwhile, the Federal Reserve's broad dollar index was 1.7% lower year over year as of 31 July. Currency translation alone is unlikely to explain the full increase; the remaining movement is consistent with, but does not identify, other demand, supply, expectations, positioning, and risk-premium channels. *(cross-exam correction 5: non-identifying residual statement.)* Two caveats bind this comparison: the horizons are mismatched (real-yield change measured since year-end; gold and dollar measured year over year), and no linear one-for-one currency effect is established. *(cross-exam correction 2: horizon limitation retained prominently.)*

Conflict and geopolitical insurance is a plausible candidate channel — and one of the least directly quantified in the present evidence set. Gold can command a safe-haven premium when investors perceive elevated risks to trade routes, energy supply, sovereign relations or financial settlement systems. Such demand is comparatively insensitive to the foregone yield on reserve assets because the objective is protection against discontinuous outcomes rather than income maximisation. The observed combination — strong gold, higher real yields and only a slightly weaker dollar — is consistent with a sizeable geopolitical premium. It is not, however, proof: the register contains no event-study measure, options-implied tail-risk series or conflict-risk index with which to isolate or rank that premium. *(cross-exam correction 6: "leading candidate" → plausible, unranked.)*

Inflation-risk and debasement concerns are a second plausible, unranked channel, more directly supported by the macro data. June headline CPI inflation was 3.46% year over year, while core CPI was 2.57% — evidence of elevated inflation persistence and uncertainty (headline CPI is not the Federal Reserve's target index, which is defined on PCE; these readings are used as persistence evidence, not a like-for-like comparison against the 2% objective). *(cross-exam correction 7: CPI vs PCE target clarified.)* Core disinflation has progressed further than headline inflation, but neither reading makes inflation risk irrelevant. The hawkish hold confirms an inflation-fighting posture, yet that posture need not be bearish for gold if investors question whether restrictive policy can restore price stability without generating fiscal, financial or growth stress. In that setting, higher real yields and stronger gold are not contradictory: both may incorporate a larger risk premium around inflation persistence and policy credibility.

Policy-error hedging is a conceptually coherent hypothesis that could weaken the negative relationship — gold can hedge both tails: renewed inflation if policy proves insufficient, and economic or financial stress if restraint proves excessive. *(cross-exam correction 6/8: "moderately persuasive" → unverified hypothesis; C7.)* Current policy-error hedge demand is not measured: neither the FOMC vote nor the rise in the two-year yield demonstrates that investors bought gold for that protection.

Official-sector absorption could contribute, particularly if central banks are diversifying reserve assets for geopolitical or settlement-risk reasons, but its current importance is unassessable — purchase data were not refreshed for this review, and no current-regime contribution is inferred. *(cross-exam correction 6/8: unweighted hypothesis; C8 UNVERIFIABLE.)* More broadly, a lasting transmission change remains a hypothesis rather than a conclusion. If real yields are rising because inflation uncertainty, term premia or sovereign-risk compensation are rising — not because the growth outlook is improving — the historical negative gold relationship may become unstable or even temporarily positive.

The confirmation framework must be designed, not assumed. The next discrete rise in real yields is one diagnostic observation, not a clean confirmation test: a single move can be confounded by simultaneous dollar, inflation, geopolitical, liquidity and policy-news changes. *(cross-exam correction 10: diagnostic, not dispositive.)* A credible test requires multiple events or an event-study design with synchronized controls; a rolling regression of gold returns on changes in the ten-year real yield and broad dollar with pre-specified windows, frequencies, lags, significance thresholds and stability tests; and horizon-matched World Gold Council official-purchase data, gold ETF creations/redemptions, and CFTC positioning to identify the marginal buyer (official, institutional or leveraged). Renewed negative sensitivity to real yields, sustained ETF outflows, weaker official purchases or a material dollar upswing accompanied by gold underperformance would weaken the stronger regime-change interpretation.

Sources are the 29 July 2026 FOMC decision and vote; FRED DGS2, DGS10 and DFII10 observations through 4 August (year-end comparators 3.47%/4.18%/1.93% radar-attributed); the LBMA PM gold fixing for 5 August; FRED DTWEXBGS through 31 July; and FRED CPIAUCSL and CPILFESL for June. Limitations are material: mismatched comparison horizons; reliance on level observations rather than a fitted return model; no refreshed ETF flows, official-sector purchases, CFTC positioning, options data, or quantified geopolitical-risk series; no fitted beta. The conclusion: the evidence is consistent with a current-regime flow-dominance hypothesis and does not establish a permanent break in the traditional real-yield channel; no flow is identified or ranked; the next real-yield move is a diagnostic observation within a pre-specified multi-event test.
