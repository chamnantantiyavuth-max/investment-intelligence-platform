**ORG-2026-0010 — Equity Analyst Verification Note**

**Verification verdict.** Apple's Q3 FY2026 Form 10-Q directly substantiates the previously derived Services gross-margin claim, as far as the reported quarterly arithmetic goes. For the quarter ended June 27, 2026, Apple disclosed Services net sales of $30,739 million and Services cost of sales of $7,494 million. The resulting gross margin is $23,245 million, or **75.6205% of Services sales**, rounding to **75.62%**. This exactly matches the Q3 FY2026 figure presented in the moat report. The comparative Q3 FY2025 disclosures — Services sales of $27,423 million and cost of sales of $6,698 million — produce a gross margin of **75.5752%**, rounding to **75.58%**, also matching the report. Accordingly, the CRO dissent's statement that Services gross margin was broadly stable around 75.6% — previously flagged as not source-cleared — is now supported at the reported quarterly level by Apple's filed category-level sales and cost lines. (cross-exam correction 2: unqualified "flattens at 75.6%" → "broadly stable year over year — 75.58% versus 75.62%, both rounding to 75.6% at one decimal"; source clearance applies to the reported margin level and the endpoint comparison only, not to causal drivers, durability, or a broader trend.) The verification is conclusive for the gross-margin arithmetic, but it does not establish category-level operating profitability or a tariff-adjusted margin.

The longer-period figures provide a useful consistency check without changing the narrow verdict. For the first nine months of FY2026, Services sales of $91,728 million and cost of sales of $21,765 million imply a gross margin of **76.27%**, compared with **75.45%** for the first nine months of FY2025. At the company level, the Q3 disclosures imply Products gross margin of approximately 40.07% and total gross margin of approximately 50.06%, consistent with Apple's reported 50.1%. These internal reconciliations reinforce that the Services calculation is based on the filed statements rather than an allocation reconstructed from third-party estimates.

Three interpretation constraints remain material. **First, Services gross margin is not Services operating margin or economic profitability.** Apple does not disclose category-level operating expenses, including the allocation of research and development, sales and marketing, corporate infrastructure, legal and regulatory costs, content investment, or other shared expenditures. The 75.62% figure therefore measures revenue less Services cost of sales only. It cannot support a conclusion that roughly three-quarters of Services revenue converts into operating income or free cash flow.

**Second, the segment allocation of the tariff refund is unknown — and that unknown sits inside the numerator of the claimed margin.** Apple stated that Q3 FY2026 total-company gross margin of 50.1% included an approximately two-percentage-point favorable tariff-refund effect and that diluted EPS included a $0.11 benefit. Without that refund, total-company gross margin would have been approximately 48.1%. Apple did not disclose whether, or to what extent, the benefit was recorded in Products cost of sales, Services cost of sales, or both. If a material portion of the refund reduced Services cost of sales, the reported 75.62% embeds a one-time benefit and overstates recurring Services gross margin — and because Services cost of sales is a fraction of Services revenue, even a consolidated-level-modest refund could move the segment margin meaningfully if concentrated there. (cross-exam/CRO tightening: the unknown allocation is an unresolved variable inside the numerator, not a peripheral caveat.) A tariff-normalized Services margin cannot be calculated from the disclosed information.

**Third, Apple does not disclose the composition underlying the category margin.** Services includes businesses with potentially different economics — such as the App Store, advertising, cloud services, content, payments, support, and licensing — but Apple does not provide their individual revenue, cost, or margin contributions. The reported stability from 75.58% to 75.62% (a ~4.5 basis-point year-over-year movement) therefore cannot be attributed reliably to pricing, scale, mix, licensing economics, App Store commissions, advertising, or cloud-cost movements. It verifies the aggregate outcome, not its drivers or durability.

The confirmation chain for prior published work is therefore precise: the moat report's previously published Q3 figures numerically reconcile to Apple's subsequently disclosed Services sales and cost-of-sales lines. (cross-exam correction 1: "derived, path-dependent estimates reconcile exactly" → "previously published Q3 figures numerically reconcile" — the reconciliation verifies the Q3 arithmetic ex post; it does not independently prove the derivation methodology of the FY2023→FY2025 annual series, which remains derived until each annual data point, classification choice, and comparability assumption is re-performed and documented.) The CRO dissent's 75.6% statement can now be upgraded from "not source-cleared" to **source-cleared as a reported quarterly gross-margin level** — the noun, not the verb: broad year-over-year stability at one-decimal precision is supported; a flattening trend claim remains an interpretation. Neither publication requires a numerical correction. Their interpretive boundaries should, however, be made explicit: the evidence confirms the reported aggregate Services gross-margin level at two Q3 endpoints, not category operating profitability, tariff-normalized margin, a multi-year trend, or the contribution of individual Services businesses.

As secondary confirmations, the same Form 10-Q strengthens several previously flagged risk claims without creating a new thesis. Apple's Item 1A risk factors expressly identify supply constraints and increasing costs for advanced semiconductors, NAND storage, and DRAM memory, and state that these trends are expected to intensify; this source-clears the memory-cost competing-claims evidence in the CRO dissent. The filing also confirms that increased demand for cloud-computing and artificial-intelligence infrastructure has produced constrained supply, extended lead times, and rising costs, including exposure through third-party cloud providers. Separately, the Supreme Court granted certiorari on June 30, 2026, in the Epic matter concerning the legal standard for civil contempt. That development confirms that the regulatory and litigation path affecting App Store economics remains active; it does not determine the ultimate commercial outcome.

**Sources and limitations.** This verification relies principally on Apple's Q3 FY2026 Form 10-Q, filed July 31, 2026, and Apple's July 30, 2026 earnings release. Calculations use disclosed figures in millions and may show immaterial rounding differences. Rigorous verdict framing: Q3 FY2025 and Q3 FY2026 Services gross-margin arithmetic is reproduced from disclosed revenue and cost lines on an unadjusted reported basis; this does not validate the moat report's annual trend, prove flattening, or establish tariff-normalized recurring margin. The conclusions are advisory research judgments limited to claim verification; they do not provide a valuation, price target, or investment recommendation.
