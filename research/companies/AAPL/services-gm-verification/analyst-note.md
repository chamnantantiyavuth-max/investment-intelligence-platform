**ORG-2026-0010 — Equity Analyst Verification Note**

**Verification verdict.** Apple's Q3 FY2026 Form 10-Q directly substantiates the previously derived Services gross-margin claim. For the quarter ended June 27, 2026, Apple disclosed Services net sales of $30,739 million and Services cost of sales of $7,494 million. The resulting gross margin is $23,245 million, or **75.6205% of Services sales**, rounding to **75.62%**. This exactly matches the Q3 FY2026 figure presented in the moat report. The comparative Q3 FY2025 disclosures — Services sales of $27,423 million and cost of sales of $6,698 million — produce a gross margin of **75.5752%**, rounding to **75.58%**, also matching the report. Accordingly, the CRO dissent's statement that "Services gross margin flattens at 75.6%," previously flagged as not source-cleared, is now supported by Apple's filed category-level sales and cost lines. The verification is conclusive for the gross-margin arithmetic, but it does not establish category-level operating profitability or a tariff-adjusted margin.

The longer-period figures provide a useful consistency check without changing the narrow verdict. For the first nine months of FY2026, Services sales of $91,728 million and cost of sales of $21,765 million imply a gross margin of **76.27%**, compared with **75.45%** for the first nine months of FY2025. At the company level, the Q3 disclosures imply Products gross margin of approximately 40.07% and total gross margin of approximately 50.06%, consistent with Apple's reported 50.1%. These internal reconciliations reinforce that the Services calculation is based on the filed statements rather than an allocation reconstructed from third-party estimates.

Three interpretation constraints remain material. **First, Services gross margin is not Services operating margin or economic profitability.** Apple does not disclose category-level operating expenses, including the allocation of research and development, sales and marketing, corporate infrastructure, legal and regulatory costs, content investment, or other shared expenditures. The 75.62% figure therefore measures revenue less Services cost of sales only. It cannot support a conclusion that roughly three-quarters of Services revenue converts into operating income or free cash flow.

**Second, the segment allocation of the tariff refund is unknown.** Apple stated that Q3 FY2026 total-company gross margin of 50.1% included an approximately two-percentage-point favorable tariff-refund effect and that diluted EPS included a $0.11 benefit. Without that refund, total-company gross margin would have been approximately 48.1%. Apple did not disclose whether, or to what extent, the benefit was recorded in Products cost of sales, Services cost of sales, or both. Consequently, the reported 75.62% Services gross margin may embed an unidentified tariff-related benefit. The filed figure is authoritative on a reported basis, but a tariff-normalized Services margin cannot be calculated from the disclosed information.

**Third, Apple does not disclose the composition underlying the category margin.** Services includes businesses with potentially different economics — such as the App Store, advertising, cloud services, content, payments, support, and licensing — but Apple does not provide their individual revenue, cost, or margin contributions. The reported stability from 75.58% to 75.62% therefore cannot be attributed reliably to pricing, scale, mix, licensing economics, App Store commissions, advertising, or cloud-cost movements. It verifies the aggregate outcome, not its drivers or durability.

The confirmation chain for prior published work is therefore clear. The moat report's Q3 figures should be regarded as **successfully verified ex post**: its derived, path-dependent estimates reconcile exactly to Apple's subsequently disclosed Services sales and cost lines. The CRO dissent's 75.6% statement can now be upgraded from "not source-cleared" to **source-cleared as a reported gross-margin claim**. Neither publication requires a numerical correction. Their interpretive boundaries should, however, be made explicit: the evidence confirms aggregate Services gross margin, not category operating profitability, tariff-normalized margin, or the contribution of individual Services businesses. This strengthens confidence in the prior arithmetic while preserving the dissent's broader distinction between high gross margin and fully demonstrated economic resilience.

As secondary confirmations, the same Form 10-Q strengthens several previously flagged risk claims without creating a new thesis. Apple's Item 1A risk factors expressly identify supply constraints and increasing costs for advanced semiconductors, NAND storage, and DRAM memory, and state that these trends are expected to intensify; this source-clears the memory-cost competing-claims evidence in the CRO dissent. The filing also confirms that increased demand for cloud-computing and artificial-intelligence infrastructure has produced constrained supply, extended lead times, and rising costs, including exposure through third-party cloud providers. Separately, the Supreme Court granted certiorari on June 30, 2026, in the Epic matter concerning the legal standard for civil contempt. That development confirms that the regulatory and litigation path affecting App Store economics remains active; it does not determine the ultimate commercial outcome.

**Sources and limitations.** This verification relies principally on Apple's Q3 FY2026 Form 10-Q, filed July 31, 2026, and Apple's July 30, 2026 earnings release. Calculations use disclosed figures in millions and may show immaterial rounding differences. The conclusions are advisory research judgments limited to claim verification; they do not provide a valuation, price target, or investment recommendation.
