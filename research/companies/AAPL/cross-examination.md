# Cross-Examination — RM-2026-0001

## Share of Mind: the essay acknowledges inference, then promotes it to a finding — **WEAKENED**

The essay says, correctly, that “Share of Mind is inferred from pricing power and sales resilience, not measured.” It nevertheless presents the dimension as established elsewhere: “Share of Mind is the entry point, and it is strong”; the thesis names it as one of the three dimensions that “carry the moat”; and the conclusion again treats it as a durable load-bearing mechanism. The supporting evidence—FY2025 revenue of $416.2B, a 46.9% consolidated gross margin, 60% indirect distribution, and 21.7% Q3 FY2026 iPhone revenue growth—is consistent with strong brand demand but does not identify brand salience, willingness to pay, consideration, or retention. Revenue growth can arise from units, mix, upgrade timing, channel movement, or comparison effects, while consolidated margin expansion is substantially influenced by Services mix. The evidence log expressly records no brand survey, retention metric, unit shipment series, or independent market-share data (§§6 and 9), and the 10-K describes highly competitive markets rather than measuring consumer preference.

The sentence “Consumers do not reassess every handset purchase from first principles” is also stated as observed behavior without evidence in the committed set. Likewise, saying that Q3 iPhone growth “contradicts any immediate brand-decay thesis” is defensible only against a severe contemporaneous demand-collapse hypothesis, not as proof that Share of Mind remained unchanged.

**Smallest correction:** describe Share of Mind as a **plausible, low-confidence mechanism consistent with commercial resilience**, not a demonstrated strong moat dimension. Replace “pricing power” with “revenue and margin resilience” unless price, unit, mix, or elasticity evidence is added.

## FY2023–FY2025 gross-margin bridge: approximately reproducible, but not uniquely attributable — **WEAKENED**

The essay claims that “roughly 1.4pp of the total-GM gain” came from Services mix and “1.4pp” from within-segment margins. The FY2025 10-K supplies enough dollar data to reproduce an approximate bridge more accurately than the rounded percentage table alone:

- FY2023 Services weight: $85,200M / $383,285M = **22.2289%**
- FY2025 Services weight: $109,158M / $416,161M = **26.2298%**
- FY2023 Products margin: $108,803M / $298,085M = **36.5007%**
- FY2023 Services margin: $60,345M / $85,200M = **70.8275%**
- FY2025 Products margin: $112,887M / $307,003M = **36.7707%**
- FY2025 Services margin: $82,314M / $109,158M = **75.4081%**

On a sequential bridge that applies the mix change at FY2023 margins and then applies margin changes at FY2025 weights, mix contributes **1.373pp** and within-segment margins contribute **1.401pp**, summing to the exact-dollar consolidated increase of **2.774pp**. This supports the rounded “1.4pp plus 1.4pp” description.

The flaw is that the attribution is path-dependent. Applying margin changes first gives **1.228pp** from within-segment margins and **1.546pp** from mix. The essay does not disclose which bridge convention it used and therefore presents one valid decomposition as though it were uniquely determined by the filing. Rounded disclosed percentages further limit precision, although the separately disclosed gross-profit dollars largely resolve that problem.

**Smallest correction:** publish the formula and ordering, state **“approximately 1.37pp mix and 1.40pp within-segment margins under a mix-first sequential bridge,”** and note that alternative ordering changes the allocation without changing the total.

## Services’ share of FY2025 gross profit — **UPHELD**

The claim that “Services generated approximately 42.2% of FY2025 gross profit” is arithmetically correct. The FY2025 10-K’s MD&A gross-margin table reports Services gross profit of **$82,314M** and total gross profit of **$195,201M**:

\[
82{,}314 / 195{,}201 = 42.1688\%
\]

That rounds to **42.2%**. Services’ revenue share is likewise $109,158M / $416,161M = **26.2298%**, or **26.2%**.

**Smallest correction:** no numerical correction is required. The essay should cite the disclosed gross-profit dollars, not merely identify the result as “Quant, derived.”

## Switching-cost strength: the mechanism is plausible, but its rank and strength are not verified — **UNVERIFIABLE**

The essay calls High Switching Cost “the strongest operating mechanism” and later treats it as one of the three dimensions carrying the moat. The listed frictions—iCloud history, paid media, accessories, watch data, passwords, messaging habits, family arrangements, and learned workflows—form a coherent mechanism, but the committed evidence does not show their magnitude or behavioral effect. The evidence log records no retention cohorts, migration rates, replacement behavior, multi-device ownership, attach rates, tenure, or switching surveys (§9); Apple’s installed-base disclosure is only an unquantified “all-time high” statement.

Services rising from 22.2% to 26.2% of revenue does not isolate switching costs. The Services category includes advertising and licensing, AppleCare, cloud, digital content, payments, App Store net commissions, and amortization of bundled-service value. Its growth can therefore reflect pricing, mix, default positions, accounting allocation, or third-party activity without any change in customer captivity. The further claim that macro weakness can “reinforce” switching costs is speculative: household replacement expense could retain users, but affordability pressure could also lengthen upgrades or encourage lower-priced alternatives.

**Smallest correction:** retain the cumulative-friction mechanism but label its **strength and rank as low-confidence and unquantified**. Replace “the strongest operating mechanism” with “a plausible candidate for the strongest operating mechanism, for which the filings provide no direct behavioral measurement.”

## Compound break scenario: coherent as a joint rent-capture stress, but not as the only moat-break mechanism — **WEAKENED**

The essay identifies “an AI-driven interface reset” combined with regulation opening Apple’s monetization control points as the most plausible break. The mechanisms can reinforce one another: an external assistant could redirect discovery and commerce away from Apple-controlled surfaces, while regulation could prevent Apple from restoring control through distribution, defaults, or payment rules. That is internally coherent as a compound **rent-capture** scenario.

The essay nevertheless conflates three propositions:

1. AI disintermediation weakens interface and discovery control.
2. Regulatory remedies weaken distribution and payment control.
3. Their combination breaks Apple’s moat.

Neither of the first two logically requires the other. Regulation could transfer App Store or payment economics while Apple remains competitive in AI; an assistant layer could capture discovery and commerce even without additional regulation. Moreover, if users retain Apple devices, brand attachment, and switching friction while Services economics weaken, the event impairs Apple’s monetization architecture without necessarily “breaking” the entire ecosystem moat. The essay recognizes this distinction elsewhere but collapses it in the thesis and conclusion.

The change-conditions are indicators, not falsification rules. The scenario needs three separately testable legs:

- **AI leg:** evidence that third-party assistants capture sustained discovery, commerce, or task initiation and that developers shift priority away from iOS-controlled interfaces.
- **Regulatory leg:** evidence that alternative distribution or payments achieve meaningful adoption across important jurisdictions and reduce Apple’s effective control or retained economics.
- **Economic outcome:** evidence that these changes reduce Services gross profit, margin, or Apple’s control of identity, orchestration, and payments rather than merely changing transaction routes.

The joint causal claim would be falsified if both interface migration and regulatory opening became material while Apple retained the relevant control points and Services economics remained durable through substitute monetization. The assertion that both legs are necessary would be falsified if either leg alone produced sustained rent erosion.

**Smallest correction:** recast the scenario as the **highest-impact joint rent-capture stress**, not the only route by which the moat can weaken; separate the two mechanisms and publish an explicit outcome test.

## Q3 FY2026 gross-margin adjustment — **UPHELD**

The Q3 FY2026 earnings release reports gross margin of **50.1%**, “including a favorable impact of approximately 2 percentage points from tariff refunds.” Subtracting the disclosed contribution gives approximately **48.1%**. The arithmetic in the essay is correct.

“Normalized margin” is stronger language than the filing supports. The subtraction produces gross margin **excluding the stated tariff-refund benefit**; it does not establish a normal steady-state margin because the evidence does not disclose the period to which the refunds relate, the underlying tariff-cost base, or whether similar refunds could recur.

**Smallest correction:** replace “normalized margin near 48.1%” with **“reported gross margin less the approximately 2pp tariff-refund benefit was about 48.1%.”**

## Nine-month OCF working-capital attribution — **UPHELD**

Nine-month OCF increased from **$81,754M to $116,996M**, a rise of **$35,242M**. The year-over-year changes in the cash-flow statement’s operating-asset and liability adjustments were:

- Accounts receivable: **+$2,631M**
- Vendor non-trade receivables: **−$7,884M**
- Inventories: **−$6,684M**
- Other current and non-current assets: **−$10,150M**
- Accounts payable: **+$13,276M**
- Other current and non-current liabilities: **+$25,177M**

These changes sum to **+$16,366M**, and $16,366M / $35,242M = **46.44%**. The essay’s “roughly $16.4B—46% of the increase” is correct.

The phrase “working-capital timing rather than earnings” is directionally reasonable but slightly overstates what the statement proves. These are changes in operating-asset and liability cash-flow adjustments; some may reflect business growth, settlement patterns, taxes, deferred items, or other operating liabilities rather than purely reversible timing.

**Smallest correction:** retain the calculation but call it **“year-over-year changes in operating-asset and liability cash-flow adjustments”** and avoid implying that every dollar is temporary.

## China figures — **UPHELD**

The FY2025 10-K reports Greater China revenue of **$72,559M in FY2023** and **$64,377M in FY2025**, against total revenue of $383,285M and $416,161M:

- FY2023: $72,559M / $383,285M = **18.9308%**, or **18.9%**
- FY2025: $64,377M / $416,161M = **15.4693%**, or **15.5%**
- FY2023–FY2025 Greater China revenue change: **−11.3%**

The Q3 FY2026 release reports Greater China revenue of **$18,816M**, versus **$15,369M** in Q3 FY2025:

\[
18{,}816 / 15{,}369 - 1 = 22.4283\%
\]

That rounds to **+22.4%**. The essay appropriately warns that one quarterly rebound does not prove structural reversal.

**Smallest correction:** no arithmetic correction is required; adding the **11.3% absolute revenue decline from FY2023 to FY2025** would make clear that the share contraction was not merely denominator growth.

## Services margin architecture as proof of moat strengthening — **WEAKENED**

The essay calls the margin architecture “the strongest quantitative evidence of a strengthening moat” and says it is the “clearest quantitative sign that Apple is converting hardware engagement into higher-margin, recurring, ecosystem-attached economics.” The figures prove that Services became a larger and more profitable reported category; they do not establish that the underlying moat strengthened, that the revenue is uniformly recurring, or that hardware engagement caused the increase.

The 10-K says FY2025 Services growth was led by advertising, the App Store, and cloud services. Note 2 also shows that Services includes heterogeneous businesses, net presentation of third-party app commissions, and amortization of service value bundled with products using estimated stand-alone selling prices. Without service-level gross profit, take rates, retention, ARPU, cohorts, or installed-base counts, the same reported result could reflect licensing/default economics, accounting mix, price changes, or regulatory-sensitive commissions.

**Smallest correction:** state that the margin architecture is **strong evidence of increasing Services mix and present monetization, but only indirect evidence consistent with—rather than proof of—a strengthening moat or deeper attachment.**

## Cost Advantage and Network Effect are described as real before being demonstrated — **UNVERIFIABLE**

The essay says “Cost Advantage is real but conditional” while also acknowledging that the filings contain no evidence Apple has the industry’s lowest unit cost. Low owned capex, outsourced manufacturing, purchase obligations, and scale show a capital-light operating model and procurement volume; they do not establish a cost advantage relative to competitors. Supplier concentration, tariff exposure, and competitors capable of operating at little or no profit are material counterevidence.

Similarly, “Network Effect is meaningful but mostly indirect” exceeds the evidence. The installed-base statement, Services revenue, and complement ecosystem are consistent with a two-sided effect, but there is no installed-base count, developer population, transaction volume, incremental-user value measure, or evidence separating network effects from integration and switching friction.

**Smallest correction:** change these conclusions to **“possible relative cost advantage, unverified against competitor unit economics”** and **“plausible indirect network effect, unquantified and not separable from integration or switching costs in the disclosed data.”**

## “None of the compound scenario’s ingredients is present” — **REFUTED**

The conclusion says that none of the compound scenario’s observable ingredients is present as of June 2026. That conflicts with the essay’s own evidence. Regulatory opening had already begun through EU alternative distribution and payment structures, the €500M European Commission fine, and U.S. restrictions involving external purchasing links. Tariff effects were also present: the 10-K described tariff costs, and Q3 FY2026 margin included refunds associated with them. Greater China had declined from $72.6B in FY2023 to $64.4B in FY2025, although the June 2026 quarter rebounded.

What is not demonstrated is AI disintermediation or the **combined scenario at moat-breaking scale**. That is materially different from saying none of its ingredients is present.

**Smallest correction:** replace the sentence with: **“Regulatory opening and tariff exposure are already observable, China evidence is mixed, and AI disintermediation is not demonstrated; the combined scenario has not materialized at a moat-breaking scale.”**

## REQUIRED essay corrections

1. Recast Share of Mind as an indirect, low-confidence hypothesis and remove the unsupported reference to measured “pricing power.”
2. Publish the gross-margin bridge formula, ordering, exact inputs, and path-dependence; use approximately **1.37pp mix plus 1.40pp within-segment margins** for the stated mix-first convention.
3. Cite the disclosed **$82,314M Services gross profit and $195,201M total gross profit** behind the correct 42.2% result.
4. Downgrade High Switching Cost from “strongest operating mechanism” to an unquantified mechanism hypothesis pending retention or migration evidence.
5. Separate AI disintermediation, regulatory opening, and economic-rent impairment into independently testable mechanisms with a defined outcome test.
6. Replace “normalized 48.1%” with “approximately 48.1% excluding the stated tariff-refund contribution.”
7. Describe the $16.4B OCF contribution as changes in operating-asset and liability adjustments, not categorically as temporary timing.
8. Reframe Services margin expansion as evidence of present mix and monetization, not direct proof of stronger switching costs, recurrence, network effects, or moat durability.
9. Correct the assertion that none of the compound scenario’s ingredients is present.
10. Change “Cost Advantage is real” and “Network Effect is meaningful” to explicitly unverified, evidence-calibrated formulations.

## Claims that should be downgraded in confidence

- “Share of Mind is strong” and is one of the three dimensions that definitively carry the moat.
- “High Switching Cost is the strongest operating mechanism.”
- Services mix and margin expansion prove increasing ecosystem attachment or a strengthening moat.
- Apple has a demonstrated relative Cost Advantage.
- Apple has a meaningful, separable Network Effect.
- The moat can “genuinely break only” through the AI-plus-regulation compound event.
- Apple would necessarily survive a normal recession, strong-dollar episode, or tariff cycle; the essay provides no explicit stress test for those counterfactuals.
- The current evidence supports high confidence in behavioral moat durability rather than high confidence only in reported economics.

## Overall assessment

The specified arithmetic largely survives scrutiny: the 42.2% Services gross-profit share, 48.1% refund-excluded Q3 margin, $16.4B working-capital contribution, and China figures are correct, while the margin bridge is approximately correct but method-dependent. The main weakness is causal calibration: the essay repeatedly converts financial outcomes consistent with Share of Mind, switching costs, network effects, and cost advantage into findings that the disclosed evidence cannot isolate or rank. After the required corrections, the essay can support strong present economics and resilience, but its conclusions about the durability and hierarchy of the underlying moat mechanisms should remain medium-to-low confidence.
