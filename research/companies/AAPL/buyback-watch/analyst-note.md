# Apple Buyback Mask Test — Analyst Note (Equity) v2 (corrected)

**Card:** ORG-2026-0007 (RADAR-001) · **Status:** post-cross-exam (corrections applied) · **Author:** Equity Analyst (org-equity-analyst) · **Date:** 2026-08-06
**Tests:** the "buyback mask" claim from `reports/apple-moat-opposing-2026-08-06.md` against Q3 FY26 data · **Evidence:** `research/companies/AAPL/buyback-watch/evidence-log.md` · **Challenge:** `research/companies/AAPL/buyback-watch/cross-examination.md`

---

**Thesis.** The 12.0% year-over-year decline in Apple's nine-month cash paid for share repurchases — from $62.094 billion in FY26 to $70.579 billion in FY25 — does not establish a 12% reduction in current buyback intent. *(cross-exam correction 1: comparative-established → leading hypothesis.)* ASR settlement timing is the leading working hypothesis for the gap, while its cause remains unresolved pending an ASR/cash bridge and full-year evidence. The contemporaneous evidence points away from retrenchment: Apple added a new $100 billion authorization in April 2026, entered into $10 billion of accelerated share repurchase agreements in May, retained $138 billion of aggregate authorization at quarter-end, and conducted approximately $25.8 billion of repurchase activity during Q3. The timing interpretation is therefore a leading but unproven working hypothesis, provisional until the Q4 ASRs settle and the FY26 full-year cash-paid figure is reported.

**The −12%: what it is and what it is not.** The radar signal measures cash paid during the first nine months of FY26, not the economic value of all repurchase transactions initiated during that period. That distinction matters because ASRs separate the up-front cash payment, initial share delivery, and final settlement across reporting periods. Cash-paid figures can consequently move sharply depending on when an ASR begins or settles, even when the underlying repurchase commitment is stable. The published moat report already documented this divergence between cash paid and the transaction value reported in the annual filing's repurchase note. *(cross-exam correction 2: timing sensitivity makes the decline non-dispositive — it does not prove timing caused it.)*

Accordingly, the decline from $70.579 billion to $62.094 billion should not be read mechanically as evidence that management reduced its intended buyback run rate by 12%. The FY25 nine-month comparator included cash effects associated with earlier ASR activity, making the year-over-year comparison vulnerable to settlement lumpiness. The measure remains relevant to the "buyback mask" question because cash paid captures the resources actually deployed over the observed interval, but it is a lagged and timing-sensitive indicator rather than a clean measure of current-period capital-allocation intent.

**Counter-evidence to durable deceleration.** Three current-quarter observations weigh against interpreting the decline as structural. First, Apple expanded rather than constrained its repurchase capacity. The company had $38.0 billion remaining under the May 1, 2025 program and added a further $100 billion authorization on April 30, 2026, leaving $138.0 billion available at June 27 — approximately 1.5 times FY25's full-year cash-paid repurchases of $90.7 billion. *(cross-exam correction 3: authorization is a capacity and board-intent signal, not a required deployment pace — it weighs modestly against an already-established retrenchment decision but cannot establish the intended deployment rate.)*

Second, Apple committed $10.0 billion up front to new ASRs in May 2026, with share delivery continuing through Q4. This is direct evidence of ongoing execution and also creates the timing mechanism likely to affect the full-year result: the final number of shares and settlement effects were not complete at the Q3 reporting date.

Third, the Item 2 repurchase table indicates substantial Q3 activity. Apple reported 79.611 million shares associated with repurchases, comprising 26.468 million shares from the May ASRs and two open-market blocks of 26.920 million and 26.223 million shares. Applying the disclosed average prices: 26.920 million × $297.18 = $8.0000856 billion and 26.223 million × $296.18 = $7.76672814 billion; adding the $10.0 billion ASR up-front payment gives an estimated Q3 total of $25.76681374 billion, or approximately $25.8 billion. *(cross-exam corrections 4+5: exact intermediates; explicitly a mixed activity estimate, not an accounting cash-paid total.)* This demonstrates substantial ongoing execution but is insufficient, by itself, to determine whether the execution pace increased or decreased relative to prior quarters — no comparable prior-quarter activity measure is presented here. *(cross-exam correction: C4 UPHELD wording.)*

**The buyback mask test.** The mask remains operative, but it did not drive the quarter's earnings result. Weighted-average basic shares declined 1.7% year over year, from 14.903 billion to 14.656 billion, while period-end issued and outstanding shares fell 1.1% from September 2025 to 14.609 billion. Repurchases therefore continued to improve per-share outcomes. Against Q3 diluted EPS growth of 28.7% and net-income growth of 27.1%, the direct EPS-versus-net-income growth spread is 1.6 percentage points; the share-count contribution is best expressed as approximately +1.3 to +1.7 percentage points (the 1.7% basic-share decline is an upper-end proxy because EPS uses diluted weighted-average shares). *(cross-exam correction 6: single-point +1.7pp replaced with the disclosed range + 1.6pp direct spread.)*

That contribution is economically visible but modest. Q3 revenue increased 16.4% and net income rose 27.1%; EPS growth was predominantly associated with net-income growth rather than share-count contraction. *(cross-exam correction 7: "predominantly organic" removed — operating and margin contributions not separately demonstrated.)* The quarter therefore leaves the CRO's claim intact — per-share resilience can outlast the organic economics funding it — but does not strengthen it. Apple is still shrinking the share base, so the mask is "on"; however, the reported earnings acceleration did not depend primarily on that mask. One strong quarter cannot either confirm or falsify the longer-horizon concern that buybacks could obscure deterioration in the underlying compounding engine.

**What will settle the question.** The decisive near-term observations are: (1) FY26 full-year cash paid for repurchases relative to FY25's $90.7 billion, (2) a reconstructed FY25–FY26 quarterly bridge covering ASR initiation, up-front cash, initial delivery, final settlement, open-market purchases, and cash-flow-statement presentation — the bridge is required to identify how much of the nine-month gap was temporal, *(cross-exam correction 8: mandatory bridge added)* and (3) authorization drawdown assessed alongside actual repurchase activity and share-count contraction. Q4 ASR settlement, combined with the reconstructed bridge, will help quantify whether the gap was temporal. *(cross-exam correction 9: "will show" → "will help quantify".)* Beyond Q4, the FY27 weighted-average and period-end share trends are the cleaner mask indicators: a flattening share count despite ample authorization would indicate the mask is thinning; continued contraction would support the settlement-timing hypothesis. The conclusion remains provisional through FY26 full-year reporting — and into FY27 share-count observation if the bridge remains incomplete. *(cross-exam correction 10.)*

**Sources and limitations.** The principal source is Apple's FY26 Q3 Form 10-Q, filed July 31, 2026, accession 0000320193-26-000020, including the Item 2 repurchase table and authorization disclosures. Historical context comes from the FY2025 Form 10-K and the prior pilot analysis. The principal limitations are the absence here of a reconstructed FY25 quarterly cash-paid and ASR-settlement bridge, the still-pending Q4 FY26 ASR settlement, and the lack of refreshed management transcript commentary. The conclusion is therefore point-in-time and conditional: ASR timing is the leading working hypothesis for the nine-month gap, durable deceleration remains unresolved rather than disproved, and the buyback mask remained modestly operative as of Q3 FY26.
