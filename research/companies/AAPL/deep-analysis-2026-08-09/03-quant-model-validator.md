# Quant & Model Validator — Earnings Quality

**Independent first-pass view · information through 2026-08-09 · portfolio-blind**

**Verdict: STRONG earnings quality, with timing and normalization caveats.** The controlling numbers are Q3 FY26 Services gross margin of **75.62%**, five-year FCF/net-income conversion of **88%–116%**, and 9M FY26 buyback cash equal to **53% of OCF**. These indicate structurally high gross economics, broadly cash-backed earnings, and better current repurchase coverage—not an accounting-led EPS story.

**Margin architecture.** Products gross margin progressed **35.3%, 36.3%, 36.5%, 37.2%, 36.8%** in FY21–FY25, then reached **40.07%** in Q3 FY26. Services moved **69.7%, 71.7%, 70.8%, 73.9%, 75.4%**, then **75.62%**. Thus, the long-run consolidated-margin lift is chiefly Services mix/margin expansion, while Products remained broadly mid-to-high-30s before the latest quarter. Q3 total gross margin was 50.1%, but approximately 2 points came from tariff refunds; refund allocation between Products and Services is undisclosed, so neither Q3 category margin is tariff-normalized. The existing Services-margin verification was **cleared for Founder review** and reproduced Q3 FY26/Q3 FY25 at 75.6205%/75.5752%; it explicitly did **not** clear the annual trend, “flattening,” category profitability, or tariff normalization. [AAPL FY2022 10-K, MD&A Gross Margin table, accession 0000320193-22-000108; evidence-log §§1, 6, 6e; services-gm-verification/final-confirmation.md]

**Below gross profit.** Operating margin was **29.8%, 30.3%, 29.8%, 31.5%, 32.0%** in FY21–FY25 and **32.6%** in Q3 FY26. Net margin was **25.9%, 25.3%, 25.3%, 24.0%, 26.9%**, then **27.2%**. FY24 comparability is poor because net income includes a one-time $10.2B Ireland State Aid tax charge; operating margin is the cleaner trend. [evidence-log §§1, 6, 7]

**Cash conversion and repurchases.** FCF/NI recomputes to **98%, 112%, 103%, 116%, 88%** for FY21–FY25; adjusting FY24 NI for the $10.2B charge reduces that year to approximately **105%**. FY25 is the watch point: NI rose 19.5% while OCF fell 5.7%, but one weaker year does not establish a persistent accrual gap. Cash buybacks consumed **83%, 73%, 70%, 80%, 81%** of OCF over FY21–FY25. In 9M FY26, cash paid fell 12.0% to $62.1B while OCF rose 43% to $117.0B, cutting coverage to 53% from 86%; fresh $10B ASRs settle through Q4, so cash-paid timing can lag economic activity. Period-end shares fell 10.1% FY21–FY25 and another 1.2% to 14.594B by 2026-07-17. No persistent accrual red flag is visible; monitor FY25 conversion, the unexplained $11.1B-to-$21.3B intangible-asset increase, and ASR settlement reconciliation.
