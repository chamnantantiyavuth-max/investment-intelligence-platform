# Internal Auditor / Red Team — Independent First Pass (RM-2026-0001)

The six-dimension framework is a useful checklist, but it is not yet a neutral research question. It presupposes that Apple possesses six identifiable moats, that they can be evaluated separately, and that their durability is the binding constraint on economic performance over the next three to five years. Those assumptions may be wrong. “Share of Mind” and “Intangible Assets” substantially overlap through brand, design reputation and trust; “Network Effect” and “High Switching Cost” can describe the same ecosystem behavior from different directions; and “Cost Advantage” or “Efficient Scale” may be consequences of bargaining power rather than independent causes. A dimension-by-dimension scorecard could therefore count one integrated system several times and manufacture confidence through taxonomy.

The better question is not simply whether users, developers and suppliers remain attached to Apple. It is: **which control points allow Apple to convert that attachment into durable incremental economic profit over the next three to five years, who could weaken or bypass those control points, and what observable evidence would show that rent capture is moving elsewhere?** This distinguishes persistence of the ecosystem from persistence of its economics. Apple could retain exceptional brand salience, a large installed base and substantial switching friction while losing the ability to monetize defaults, distribution, payments or premium hardware differentiation.

The current evidence base cannot answer that stronger question. It is overwhelmingly issuer-produced and therefore optimized for financial reporting, not moat falsification. The FY2025 10-K discloses $19.524 billion of selling and marketing expense for the year ended September 27, 2025, but not brand advertising, customer-acquisition spending, channel incentives or promotional support separately [Apple FY2025 10-K, accession 0000320193-25-000079, Note 13]. Consequently, strong “Share of Mind” cannot be treated as costless organic preference. The filings also provide no device-level retention, migration, replacement-cycle, cohort, subscriber or ARPU data. The Q3 FY2026 statement that the active-device installed base reached an all-time high across every major category and geography is qualitative and supplies neither the count nor the definition of “active” [Apple Q3 FY2026 8-K Exhibit 99.1, accession 0000320193-26-000018, quarter ended June 27, 2026].

Services evidence is especially vulnerable to category error. FY2025 Services revenue was $109.158 billion, or 26.2% of total revenue, and reported Services gross margin was 75.4% [Apple FY2025 10-K, year ended September 27, 2025, Note 2 and MD&A]. Those figures show attractive reported economics, but not why customers stay or how defensible each service is. Services combines advertising and third-party licensing, AppleCare, cloud, digital content, payments, App Store commissions and amortization of value bundled into product sales. For iCloud, Siri and Maps bundled with hardware, Apple lacks observable stand-alone prices and allocates revenue using estimated stand-alone selling prices before recognizing it over the estimated service period; third-party App Store sales are reported net, with only Apple’s retained commission recognized [Apple FY2025 10-K, Note 2]. Growth in this aggregate can therefore reflect accounting allocation, mix and default-position economics rather than paid adoption or a strengthening network effect. A credible analysis needs gross profit and retention economics by service, take rates, default-search or licensing dependence, developer multi-homing and the sensitivity of revenue to regulatory remedies—none is supplied here.

Capital returns create another optical hazard. Apple spent approximately $438.6 billion on repurchases over FY2021–FY2025 while period-end shares declined 10.1%, according to the committed evidence log’s XBRL series; the FY2025 10-K separately reports $89.3 billion spent to repurchase 402 million shares during the year ended September 27, 2025 [Apple FY2025 10-K, Note 16]. Repurchases can improve per-share results and reduce stated equity without demonstrating stronger customer captivity, innovation or industry structure. The eventual essay must separate operating-profit durability from financial engineering and avoid using EPS resilience, book-equity contraction or cash deployment as circular proof of a moat.

The framework can fail most dramatically if the locus of value moves. An AI assistant could become the primary interface through which users discover information, transact and select applications. Apple might retain its devices, brand and switching costs yet cede differentiation and economics to model providers, cloud infrastructure or cross-platform agents. Conversely, Apple could remain the access gate but incur growing inference or partnership costs that compress the rent attached to that gate. Regulation could require alternative stores, payment paths, default choice or interoperability: the installed base would remain, but the tollbooth economics could weaken. Share of mind could stay high while replacement cycles lengthen; developer participation could remain high while economically important activity migrates to the web or AI agents; reported margins could rise through Services mix even as hardware cost advantage deteriorates. None of these outcomes is well predicted by awarding six static moat grades.

Process integrity therefore requires explicit disconfirmation rules before narrative synthesis. The cell should seek third-party evidence on retention and switching, replacement periods, market share by price tier, developer economics, supplier bargaining, service take rates, default-setting revenue, regulatory remedies and AI usage—not merely corroborating quotations from Apple. Claims such as “ecosystem lock-in,” “network effect” or “pricing power” must be labeled as hypotheses unless supported by observable behavior. The strongest counter-case must appear in full: Apple remains culturally and operationally dominant, yet value capture migrates to another layer. Any essay that begins with the 10-K’s vocabulary, treats issuer-reported scale as proof, hides unavailable data, presents unverifiable claims as fact, or defines break conditions only after seeing results should be considered untrustworthy.

Apple’s ecosystem may be durable, but ecosystem persistence and economic-rent persistence are not the same proposition. The committed filings establish scale, mix and reported profitability while leaving the decisive behavioral and control-point evidence largely undisclosed. The research should therefore test where value is captured and how that capture could migrate, rather than merely scoring six overlapping labels.
