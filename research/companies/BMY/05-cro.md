# CRO — Challenger View (BMY)

**Date:** 2026-08-11 · **Mandate:** RM-2026-0006

## The thesis to attack

"BMY = real operational pivot (Growth Portfolio >50%, +15%) + guidance raise + cheap multiple (~9.4x forward) — the strongest inflection candidate of the 4."

## Challenge 1 — "Growth Portfolio +15%" flatters; the denominator is a cliff

Growth Portfolio $7.6B (+15%) vs Legacy $5.4B (−4%): the crossover is real, but Legacy is **$5.4B/quarter = $21.6B/year of revenue STILL SHRINKING** (Revlimid −49%, Pomalyst −71%). Eliquis +7% demand is a **pre-generic reprieve** — US exclusivity loss is pending; when Eliquis goes generic (the single biggest Legacy line at $4,481M/qtr), the −4% Legacy decline accelerates to double-digits. The Growth Portfolio +15% may NOT outrun the Legacy decline at that point. "Pivot working" is true TODAY; "durable" is unproven.

## Challenge 2 — Cheap is cheap for a reason

~9.4x forward non-GAAP. The market is not stupid: it prices the Eliquis LOE cliff + milvexian binary + non-GAAP opacity. "Cheap" without the catalyst being PREFUNDED is a value trap framing. The report must not present "cheap" as an edge — it must show why the market's discount is WRONG.

## Challenge 3 — OCF $4,497M on NI $5,994M H1 (75% conversion) is weak

The quant view's "cheap" rests on non-GAAP EPS quality. H1 OCF/NI = 75% — below pharma norms (ABBV ~85%+). Working-capital timing or real collection issues? Not yet explained. If the 10-Q shows receivables ballooning, the non-GAAP "quality" thesis weakens.

## Challenge 4 — No buyback = no EPS backstop

Dividend-only return ($2,570M H1) while debt repayments $1,720M — management is DELEVERING, not returning capital aggressively. That's conservative (fine) but means there's no buyback cushion if guidance slips; the EPS story is 100% operational — double-edged.

## Challenge 5 — FY2024 loss year: is non-GAAP "clean" believable?

FY2024 −$8.9B GAAP vs what non-GAAP? If the non-GAAP series stayed positive through the charge years, then non-GAAP was doing a LOT of excluding. The add-back schedule needs the same scrutiny as ABBV — a company that lost $8.9B GAAP in a year and reported "adjusted earnings" positive is aggressively filtering. The +40% non-GAAP growth must be read against THAT filter.

## What the essay must do

1. Quantify the Eliquis LOE cliff explicitly (the $4,481M/qtr line that will go generic) — the single biggest risk.
2. Present "cheap" WITH the reason (market pricing the cliff) — and argue why that's wrong ONLY with evidence.
3. Explain OCF/NI 75% (working capital? timing?).
4. Label non-GAAP add-backs (post-$8.9B-loss-year filter).
5. State that buyback is absent — no EPS backstop.

**CRO stance:** The pivot is real and the best of the 4 — but the report must NOT call it "cheap with a working pivot" without confronting the Eliquis cliff head-on. Publishable frame: "real pivot, deep value, ONE giant overhang (Eliquis LOE) that determines whether the multiple re-rates or the discount persists."
