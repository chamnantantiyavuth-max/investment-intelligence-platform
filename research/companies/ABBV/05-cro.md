# CRO — Challenger View (ABBV)

**Date:** 2026-08-11 · **Mandate:** RM-2026-0005 · **Role:** independent dissent — attack the thesis BEFORE it is written

## The thesis to attack

"The ABBV EPS inflection is real (normalized earnings recovery + durable immunology growth), delivered at S2-early."

## Challenge 1 — "Real growth" is partly a re-labeling

Immunology +15.1% reported / +14.6% operational — but Skyrizi's US growth is now comping against its own launched base; Rinvoq faces JAK-class FDA boxed-warning overhang and CVS/Caremark formulary pressure (2025 exclusions were a known risk; Rinvoq was on the 2025 exclusion list and clawed back — the next cycle could repeat). "Durable" needs proof, not assertion. The +8.7% FY2025 revenue is flattered by price increases in a deflationary-cost drug environment — volume growth is the real test and is not separately disclosed per product.

## Challenge 2 — The +290% EPS is a low-base artifact, and the "adjusted" number is management-defined

Q2 GAAP +290% is meaningless as a growth signal. Adjusted +22.9% is the company's own filter, not independently verified. H1 GAAP net income $4,313M ≈ FY2025 full year is only "inflection" if you believe the FY2023–25 depression was entirely charges — what if part of it was REAL erosion (Humira $12B→$756M, oncology −1.5%) that the charges conveniently masked? The "clean" adjusted series is exactly the series management wants you to see.

## Challenge 3 — Stage S2-early is a snapshot, and the multiple is not cheap

At $247.97 with adjusted TTM EPS ~$14, P/E ≈ 17.7x — reasonable, NOT a discount. The whole "inflection" premium story depends on ~15% growth compounding for years. If Skyrizi/Rinvoq decelerate toward low-teens (biosimilar pressure on Rinvoq? new entrants?), 17.7x on decelerating growth is not asymmetric — it's fair-to-rich. Where is the margin of safety?

## Challenge 4 — The August 2026 debt deal is a canary, not a footnote

Why does a company with $5.2B cash, ~$17.8B FCF, and negligible buybacks price debt in August 2026? Options: (a) Apogee pre-funding (plausible, but Apogee is a biotech with no marketed product — paying up for pipeline), (b) refinancing (fine), (c) **balance-sheet stress the guidance doesn't show** (undiscussed). The report must NOT wave this away; it must present (a)-(c) with the evidence status of each. My read: the burden is on the bulls to explain why a cash-generative pharma needs new debt at 6%+ long-end coupons (per GOOGL context, long tenors are rich).

## Challenge 5 — Dividend-led capital return is a constraint

~66% FCF payout to dividends leaves ~34% for everything else (Apogee + debt service + M&A). The dividend is the "bond-proxy" bid; if growth disappoints, the yield supports price but the total-return case weakens. There is no buyback cushion (unlike the Apple case) to offset EPS dilution from the Apogee stock component.

## What the essay must do (my minimum)

1. Quantify the low-base effect explicitly (GAAP +290% vs adjusted +22.9%; H1 = FY25 full year).
2. Prove growth durability with volume/market-share evidence, not just reported $ growth (if unavailable — say so).
3. Treat the debt deal as a flagged uncertainty with 3 hypotheses, not a footnote.
4. State the P/E basis explicitly (17.7x adjusted TTM) and whether the inflection premium is already paid.
5. NOT present stage S2-early as a reason to buy — it is a reason to research.

**CRO stance:** I oppose publishing a report that calls this a durable step-change. A report that honestly frames "low-base recovery + real-but-unproven-durability immunology growth, fair multiple, one unexplained debt deal" is publishable. The difference is material.
