# CASE-2026-001 (UNH) · B1 synthesis · Evidence Gap EG-2026-001-B1-01

Status: research synthesis, not a conclusion. Every finding is canonically UNVERIFIED. The Evidence Lead re-fetched every source and reproduced every CMS-derived number, but nothing has been promoted to VERIFIED, and no Scuttlebutt, Audit, Underwriting or Founder-ready stage is marked complete. SUPPORTED, INFERRED and UNRESOLVED are prose labels only. Sources were public only: CMS MA Landscape, Plan Crosswalk and PBP files, and SEC EDGAR filings. There were no interviews, surveys, field research or MNPI. As of 2026-10-18.

## Question
Was UnitedHealth's 2026 Medicare Advantage membership contraction accompanied by a disproportionate reduction in provider-network availability or benefit coverage versus HUM, CVS/Aetna, ELV and CNC?

## Short answer
Mostly no. On footprint, network proxies and most benefit measures, UNH's 2026 cuts were inside the peer range, usually close to CVS/Aetna. The clear exception is premiums: UNH raised premiums on a larger share of its plans than any peer did. UNH's own filings attribute the contraction to its "benefit and pricing actions". The evidence therefore points to deliberate pricing and benefit design plus share loss to Humana, not to a uniquely large footprint or network pullback. This is plan-count evidence, not enrollment-weighted.

## Per subquestion

**SQ1. Service-area footprint: not disproportionate (falsifier met), with adverse sub-measures.**
- SUPPORTED: Plan-county pairs fell 6.5% at UNH against a peer median of −9.15% (HUM +16.8%, CVS −7.6%, ELV −15.2%, CNC −10.7%). Excluding SNPs, UNH fell 11.9% against a peer median of −17.75%. Plans terminated or with a reduced service area were 24.3% at UNH against a peer median of 31.05%. Source: CMS CY2025 vs CY2026 MA Landscape and the 2025→2026 Plan Crosswalk. (IE-SQ1-01, -03)
- Adverse, SUPPORTED: Counties served fell 3.8% at UNH against a peer median of −1.5%. Plan count fell 8.0% against a peer median of −6.3%. UNH exited 131 counties and entered 22. (IE-SQ1-02)
- INFERRED: UNH pruned plans and edge counties but kept its core footprint relatively intact. (IE-SQ1-04)

**SQ2. Benefit coverage: mostly within the peer range. Premiums are outside it.**
- SUPPORTED, within range: The median MOOP change was $0 at UNH, and 45.5% of UNH plans raised MOOP (peers 14.3–63.7%). The $0-premium share moved −3.8 pts (peers −5.2 to +0.4). 98.4% of UNH plans raised the Part D deductible (peers 39–99%), largely because the IRA standard deductible went from $590 to $615. (CP-SQ2-01, -02, -04)
- Adverse, SUPPORTED: 28.0% of UNH plans raised premiums against 6.0–19.8% at peers, with a mean of +$5.47 against −$1.26 to +$3.44. (CP-SQ2-03)
- Supplementals: 32.1% of UNH plans dropped dental, eyewear or OTC, the highest of the five and just above CVS at 31.2% (SUPPORTED, adverse, CP-SQ2-A1). Including SNPs, UNH is 25.8% against CVS 25.9%. Counting benefit drops or dollar-maximum cuts, UNH is 62.0% against a peer range of 11.3–81.6%, inside the range. Comprehensive dental, OTC and eyewear measures are each inside the range. (CP-SQ2-A2 to A4)
- INFERRED: UNH's supplemental cuts are at the top of the peer range, level with CVS, and not uniquely disproportionate. (CP-SQ2-A-SUMMARY)
- UNRESOLVED: Flex-card values, because PBP has no flex-card field. On the SSBCI proxy UNH removed none, but confidence is low because CMS ended its VBID model for 2026. OTC dollar amounts are also unresolved because the field is mostly blank. (CP-SQ2-A5)

**SQ3. Provider network: no disproportionate narrowing found (falsifier provisionally met).**
- INFERRED, from absence of evidence: UNH's FY2025 10-K and Q2 2026 10-Q contain only generic network-risk language. They disclose no specific 2026 terminations, PPO-to-HMO conversions or adequacy actions. (CP-SQ3-01)
- SUPPORTED: As a PPO plan-county proxy, UNH fell 15.2% against CVS −20.8%, ELV −39.0% and CNC −32.5%, while HUM rose 20.3%. This proxy is not proof of conversions and overlaps the SQ1 footprint data. (CP-SQ3-02)
- UNRESOLVED: Peer 2026 network narrowing, because peer FY2025 10-Ks are generic too. (CP-SQ3-03)

**SQ4. Attribution and peer context: UNH points to its own pricing and benefit actions.**
- SUPPORTED: UNH's 2026 10-Qs attribute the MA contraction to "benefit and pricing actions". The "exit certain markets" language belongs to the Optum value-based-care paragraph, not Medicare Advantage. The reported 1.1M/1.6M decline covers all of UHC, not MA alone. UNH MA membership went from 8,445k (Dec 2025) to 7,555k (Mar 2026, −10.5%) and then 7,605k (Sep 30, 2026). (IE-SQ4-01 to -03)
- SUPPORTED, peer context: HUM grew 22.6% despite cutting counties. CVS fell 2.2% despite deeper cuts. ELV fell 15.8% and calls this "deliberate repositioning"; ELV also disclosed a CMS sanction concerning risk-adjustment data submission. CNC is flat, but that figure is distorted by MMP reclassification. (IE-SQ4-04 to -06)
- INFERRED: Benefit and premium design, plus share loss to Humana, explain UNH's loss better than footprint does. (IE-SQ4-07)

## Contradictory evidence kept
- UNH's county exits (−3.8%) and plan-count cut (−8.0%) were deeper than the peer medians.
- UNH had the highest share of plans raising premiums, and the highest share dropping a core supplemental (narrowly, versus CVS).
- CVS made footprint and benefit cuts similar to or deeper than UNH's yet lost much less membership. That suggests price positioning or competitive dynamics matter, not just the scale of the cuts.

## Limits and open questions
- Nothing is enrollment-weighted. That needs the CMS monthly contract/plan/county enrollment file.
- Survivorship bias: the benefit comparisons use continuing plans only. Plans were matched by ID, not through the crosswalk.
- The CY2025 baseline is a mid-year Landscape revision. Membership comparison periods differ across issuers. ELV's Dec 2025 and CNC's MA-only figures were not extracted.
- Not fetched: CMS non-renewal notices, CMS network-adequacy actions, earnings-call transcripts, UNH 8-Ks, UNH Q3 2026 materials, and peer 2026 10-Q network commentary.
- Open: Did UNH's premium increases land in markets where Humana gained share? Are UNH's network changes visible in CMS provider directories or adequacy data?
