## 5. Lessons

- **A vendor field value is not a point-in-time fact.** Trailing EPS and moving averages from the same source can differ between two fetches of the *same* closed session. Record `_fetched_at` next to every field value, and never compare a field across logs without it.
- **A same-PIT re-fetch is a cheap, high-value data-quality probe.** Because no session intervened, *any* field difference is attributable to the vendor, not the market. This fire isolated the instability to MA windows + trailing EPS while prices stayed byte-identical — a cleaner attribution than any price-move analysis could give.
- **Treat "vs 50d / vs 200d" as an order-of-magnitude read, not a measurement.** A ~0.8% MA recompute noise is larger than many of the week-over-week differences the series has been narrating.
- **Calendar-first remains the cheapest discriminator.** One calendar call for 9 tickers again converted a batch of field moves from "possible fundamental events" into "definite field defects."
- **Report the window length and the fetch provenance together.** This fire's window is 14 days *and* its fields are a second observation of the same PIT; both facts are needed before any number here is compared with the prior logs.
- **State a same-day double fire explicitly.** A schedule change that produces two fires in one day is only visible in cron state; the SRL is the only place the series can carry it.
