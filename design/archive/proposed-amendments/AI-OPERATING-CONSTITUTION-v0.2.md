# AI Operating Constitution

## 1. Purpose

This Constitution governs the role of artificial intelligence inside the Investment Intelligence Platform after the platform becomes an application.

AI is not limited to assisting software development. It may operate as an embedded intelligence capability inside approved product workflows, subject to the boundaries defined below.

The platform shall preserve a clear separation among:

1. deterministic computation and authoritative system state;
2. AI-assisted interpretation and reasoning;
3. human-authorized decisions.

No implementation may collapse these three responsibilities into a single opaque AI-controlled process.

---

## 2. Three-Layer Authority Model

The platform shall be designed around three distinct layers.

### 2.1 Deterministic Core

The Deterministic Core is authoritative for functions that require reproducibility, exact calculation, rule enforcement, validation, state transitions, access control, or auditability.

It includes, where applicable:

- mathematical and financial calculations;
- data validation and normalization;
- approved screening and ranking rules;
- lifecycle and governance-state enforcement;
- permissions and approval gates;
- timestamps, versions, identifiers, and lineage;
- storage and retrieval of authoritative records;
- deterministic alerts and threshold checks;
- audit logs and change history.

A language model shall not be treated as the authoritative calculator, database, rules engine, permission system, or source of governance truth.

### 2.2 AI Intelligence Layer

The AI Intelligence Layer may perform work that requires interpretation, synthesis, comparison, classification, hypothesis generation, explanation, critique, or natural-language interaction.

Permitted functions may include:

- summarizing source material;
- extracting claims and candidate facts;
- analyzing businesses, industries, themes, macro conditions, catalysts, and risks;
- comparing companies, products, assets, and hypotheses;
- generating bull, base, and bear cases;
- challenging assumptions and identifying contradictions;
- explaining changes in scores, evidence, or thesis state;
- drafting research reports and decision-support material;
- answering questions over approved evidence;
- proposing further research;
- monitoring new information and assessing its possible relevance.

AI output remains non-authoritative until processed according to its required validation and review status.

### 2.3 Founder Approval Layer

The Founder remains the final authority for material investment judgment, approval, rejection, promotion, override, and strategy use.

AI may recommend, warn, challenge, prioritize, summarize, or draft.

AI may not independently:

- approve an investment;
- authorize capital allocation;
- place or direct a trade;
- promote experimental intelligence into official strategy output;
- alter approved policy or scoring semantics;
- override the Founder;
- erase, rewrite, or conceal prior evidence or decisions;
- silently convert uncertainty into fact;
- represent an unreviewed hypothesis as approved knowledge.

---

## 3. AI Inside Application Functions

Application functions shall be explicitly classified as one of the following:

1. **Deterministic**
2. **AI-Assisted**
3. **Hybrid**
4. **Human-Authorized**

Each material function contract shall identify:

- its classification;
- authoritative inputs;
- deterministic calculations;
- AI responsibilities;
- validation requirements;
- required evidence;
- approval requirements;
- failure behavior;
- persisted outputs;
- audit and provenance requirements.

No material function may use AI merely because AI is available. AI shall be used only where interpretation or reasoning adds justified value.

---

## 4. Hybrid Workflow Principle

Where a workflow combines software and AI, deterministic operations shall remain separate from AI interpretation.

A preferred workflow pattern is:

1. acquire approved source data;
2. validate freshness, identity, completeness, and provenance;
3. compute deterministic metrics;
4. provide validated evidence and metrics to AI;
5. allow AI to interpret within an approved workflow;
6. validate the AI output structure and evidence references;
7. record uncertainty and review status;
8. present the result for required human review or approval;
9. preserve the resulting snapshot and decision lineage.

AI shall not silently substitute its own calculations for deterministic outputs.

---

## 5. Evidence and Epistemic Status

Every material AI-generated output shall preserve its epistemic status.

AI extraction, classification, inference, hypothesis, interpretation, and recommendation shall not automatically become an Observed Fact.

Where material, AI output shall include or link to:

- source references;
- evidence identifiers;
- as-of time;
- workflow version;
- model or model-class provenance;
- prompt or instruction-set version where retained by policy;
- uncertainty or confidence representation;
- review status;
- supersedes or superseded-by lineage;
- limitations and missing evidence.

Acceptance of an AI-derived record does not retroactively make the original AI process infallible.

---

## 6. Reproducibility and Versioning

Material AI workflows shall be versioned.

The platform shall preserve sufficient provenance to explain:

- what information was available;
- which deterministic rules were applied;
- which AI workflow was used;
- what output was produced;
- who reviewed or approved it;
- what later changed.

The platform is not required to guarantee identical natural-language output from every model execution. It is required to preserve authoritative inputs, workflow identity, review state, and decision lineage.

---

## 7. Model Neutrality

The product architecture shall not depend constitutionally on a specific AI provider, model name, terminal agent, or development tool.

GPT, Claude, Gemini, Hermes, local models, or future systems may serve as replaceable execution components.

The Constitution, evidence model, workflow contracts, authority boundaries, and stored project knowledge shall remain independent from the selected model wherever practical.

Changing the model shall not automatically change:

- approved investment doctrine;
- scoring semantics;
- governance rules;
- evidence requirements;
- Founder authority;
- official strategy state.

---

## 8. Failure and Degraded Operation

AI unavailability shall not corrupt authoritative system state.

Every material AI-assisted function shall define a failure mode appropriate to its role, which may include:

- retry;
- queue for later processing;
- fall back to a different approved model;
- return a clearly marked incomplete result;
- require manual review;
- disable only the affected intelligence function.

The application shall not fabricate completion when an AI task fails.

Where feasible, deterministic and recordkeeping functions shall remain operable without AI.

---

## 9. Cost, Latency, and Model Routing

Cost, latency, routing, retries, context management, and provider selection are implementation concerns governed by approved design documents rather than permanent constitutional doctrine.

However, implementation choices shall not weaken:

- evidence quality;
- authority boundaries;
- auditability;
- privacy controls;
- human approval gates;
- disclosure of incomplete or failed analysis.

A cheaper or faster model shall not be silently substituted where it fails an approved quality requirement.

---

## 10. Privacy and Data Boundaries

AI workflows shall receive only information permitted by approved data, privacy, licensing, and security policies.

Sensitive, private, broker, portfolio, or proprietary information shall not be sent to an external model merely because the application can technically do so.

Each integration shall respect:

- data classification;
- provider retention policy;
- approved purpose;
- minimum necessary disclosure;
- access control;
- logging and audit requirements.

---

## 11. Monitoring Agents

AI may monitor approved information sources and generate alerts, proposed updates, contradictions, or research tasks.

Monitoring authority is not decision authority.

An AI monitoring agent may:

- detect new evidence;
- compare it with prior research;
- propose that a thesis or Theme be reviewed;
- prepare a change summary;
- escalate uncertainty or risk.

It may not autonomously convert a proposed change into an approved investment decision, strategy rule, or capital action.

---

## 12. Specialized Agents and Delegation

The platform may use one or more specialized AI agents, subagents, tools, or workflow roles where specialization provides justified value.

A logical responsibility does not automatically require a separately deployed agent. The implementation shall use the smallest structure that can satisfy the approved function, evidence, quality, audit, security, and cost requirements.

Before a material specialized agent is authorized for implementation, its governing contract shall define:

- purpose and bounded responsibility;
- authority boundary;
- permitted evidence and data access;
- prohibited actions;
- authoritative inputs;
- deterministic dependencies;
- output contract;
- evidence and provenance requirements;
- validation and quality checks;
- uncertainty and review status;
- failure and degraded-operation behavior;
- escalation path;
- human or Founder approval requirements;
- retention and correction behavior.

An AI agent may delegate work only within authority already granted by approved system policy.

AI-to-AI delegation shall not:

- create additional authority;
- bypass a human or Founder approval gate;
- promote an unreviewed output into authoritative state;
- widen data access without authorization;
- alter investment doctrine, scoring semantics, or governance rules;
- conceal the origin of an output;
- erase disagreement or dissent among agents.

Where multiple agents contribute to one output, the system shall preserve sufficient lineage to identify:

- which agent or workflow role produced each material contribution;
- which evidence and deterministic outputs it used;
- where agents agreed or disagreed;
- how conflicts were resolved or presented;
- which component synthesized the final result;
- who reviewed or approved the result.

Specialized-agent names, quantity, orchestration framework, model assignment, execution topology, and routing policy are design decisions and shall not be fixed by this Constitution.

---

## 13. Application Design Requirement

Before implementation of a material product workflow, the design phase shall define an **AI Application Function Contract** or equivalent record.

At minimum, the contract shall answer:

1. What part is deterministic?
2. What part uses AI?
3. What evidence may AI access?
4. What output is authoritative?
5. What validation is required?
6. What requires Founder or human approval?
7. What happens when AI fails?
8. What provenance is stored?
9. Can the model be replaced without changing domain meaning?
10. How is the output corrected without erasing history?

---

## 14. Correction Doctrine

Material AI-generated records shall not be silently overwritten to hide prior error.

Corrections shall preserve the original record, correction reason, replacement output, actor, timestamp, and relevant workflow version according to the approved evidence and audit doctrine.

Where an AI output influenced a human decision, later correction of the AI output shall not erase the historical decision context.

---

## 15. Constitutional Summary

The Investment Intelligence Platform shall follow this permanent operating principle:

> Data and deterministic calculations establish reproducible system truth.  
> AI interprets, synthesizes, challenges, and assists.  
> The Founder authorizes material investment decisions.

The application may embed AI deeply in its functions, but AI shall remain an accountable intelligence layer rather than an unbounded source of truth or authority.
